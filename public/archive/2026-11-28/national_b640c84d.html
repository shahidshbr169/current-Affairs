Proposed 50% increase in Lok Sabha seats raises concerns over representation of southern States: Rajat Ullagaddimath

28 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Hubballi‑Dharwad urban district committee chair, Rajat Ullagaddimath, warned that a uniform 50% increase in Lok Sabha seats could distort the relative political representation of the southern states, raising concerns over equitable federal balance.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; Indian Federalism; Delimitation & Representation.

Key Data & Stats: 50% increase in total Lok Sabha seats | Article 81(1) – composition of Lok Sabha | 84th Amendment (2001) – freeze on seat allocation till 2026

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Lok Sabha currently has 543 elected members. Periodic delimitation exercises, governed by the Delimitation Commission, adjust seat distribution based on census data. The 84th Amendment froze the number of seats per state after the 1971 census, postponing any increase until after 2026. Recent proposals in Parliament aim to raise the total strength by 50% to accommodate population growth and improve representation.
  • Significance: A uniform hike would alter the balance of power among states, potentially marginalising smaller or less‑populous southern states. This impacts federal equity, resource allocation, and the political weight of regional parties, which are pivotal in coalition governments.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Constitutional amendment required under Article 81 and Article 368.
    • Risk of violating the principle of “one person, one vote” if seat increase is not proportionate to population changes.
    • Political resistance from states fearing loss of influence, especially in the South and Northeast.
    • Logistical issues: redrawing constituency boundaries, updating electoral rolls, and ensuring compliance with the Representation of the People Act.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a high‑level committee (e.g., chaired by a former Chief Election Commissioner) to examine demographic trends and recommend a formula‑based seat increase.
    • Refer to the Sarkaria Commission’s guidelines on centre‑state balance before any amendment.
    • Consider a phased increase, prioritising states with the highest population growth while preserving a minimum seat floor for smaller states.
    • Engage all state governments through the Inter‑State Council to build consensus and avoid polarisation.

Prelims Pointers

  • Article 81(1) – composition of Lok Sabha and criteria for seat allocation.
  • Delimitation Commission – constitutional body responsible for redrawing constituencies.
  • 84th Amendment (2001) – freeze on seat allocation based on 1971 census, effective till 2026.
  • Principle of “one person, one vote” – fundamental to equitable representation.
