Hasina has become a ‘thorn’ in Bangladesh-India ties: Minister Humaiun Kobir

28 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: State Minister for Foreign Affairs Humaiun Kobir warned that the continued presence of Bangladesh’s former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina in India is straining bilateral ties, signalling a diplomatic friction point between New Delhi and Dhaka.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; India’s foreign policy; India‑South Asia relations.

Key Data & Agreements: No specific treaty cited

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The remark underscores a subtle shift in Bangladesh’s diplomatic posture, reflecting concerns over perceived Indian interference and the broader contest for influence in the Bay of Bengal and South‑Asian security architecture.
  • Impact on India: Potential erosion of goodwill may affect cooperation on critical issues such as the Teesta water sharing, cross‑border trade, and joint infrastructure projects (e.g., the India‑Bangladesh Friendship Bridge), while also providing an opening for rival powers to deepen engagement with Dhaka.
  • Way Forward: India should pursue calibrated diplomatic outreach—high‑level visits, confidence‑building measures, and reaffirmation of existing agreements—to mitigate mistrust, while leveraging multilateral platforms like SAARC and BIMSTEC for collective dialogue.

Prelims Pointers

  • Bangladesh and India share a 4,096 km land border and a 54‑km maritime boundary in the Bay of Bengal.
  • Key bilateral frameworks include the 1996 Land Boundary Agreement and the 2015 India‑Bangladesh Protocol on Water Sharing (Teesta).
  • Both nations are members of regional groupings: SAARC, BIMSTEC, and the G20 (as a dialogue partner for Bangladesh).
  • Recent diplomatic friction has coincided with increased Chinese economic outreach to Bangladesh, raising strategic concerns for New Delhi.
