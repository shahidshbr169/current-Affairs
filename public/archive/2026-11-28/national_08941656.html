Mayawati supports women’s reservation Bill, seeks separate quota for SC, ST women

28 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Uttar Pradesh chief minister Mayawati endorsed the Women’s Reservation Bill and urged that a distinct quota be carved out for women belonging to Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST), arguing that their historic marginalisation warrants separate safeguards.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; Articles on reservation, affirmative action, and gender equality.

Key Data & Stats: No specific quantitative data released in the statement

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Women’s Reservation Bill (proposed 108th Constitutional Amendment) seeks to reserve 33 % seats for women in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. Past attempts (1996, 1998, 2008) have stalled. SC/ST women remain under‑represented even within existing reservation quotas, prompting calls for a “double‑layered” reservation.
  • Significance: Introducing a separate SC/ST women quota would address intersectional discrimination, enhance descriptive representation, and could improve policy outcomes for marginalized communities. It also tests the flexibility of India’s affirmative‑action framework.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Constitutional validity: Must reconcile with Articles 15(4) & 16(4) (allowing special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes) and the “basic structure” doctrine upheld in *Indra Sawhney v. Union of India* (1992).
    • Potential dilution of existing SC/ST quotas and intra‑group competition for limited seats.
    • Political consensus: The Bill has faced opposition from parties fearing loss of electoral arithmetic.
    • Implementation logistics: Identifying eligible candidates, preventing proxy nominations, and ensuring compliance at the grassroots.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a high‑level committee (e.g., under the Chairmanship of the Chief Justice or a former Union Minister) to examine the legal feasibility of a “double reservation” model.
    • Seek advisory input from the Law Commission and the National Commission for Women on gender‑sensitive reservation design.
    • Pilot the separate SC/ST women quota in Panchayati Raj Institutions (leveraging the 73rd & 74th Amendments) before scaling to Parliament and State Assemblies.
    • Engage civil society and academic experts to assess impact on political representation and social empowerment.

Prelims Pointers

  • Women’s Reservation Bill – proposed 108th Constitutional Amendment to reserve 33 % seats for women in Lok Sabha & State Assemblies.
  • Constitutional provisions relevant: Art. 15(4), Art. 16(4), Art. 335 (SC/ST interests).
  • Key Supreme Court precedent: Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992) – “basic structure” test for reservation policies.
  • Existing reservation framework: SC – 15 %, ST – 7.5 % in Parliament; women’s reservation limited to local bodies (73rd & 74th Amendments).
