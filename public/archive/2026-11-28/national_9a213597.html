Keralam govt. examines demand for action against doctors prescribing unsafe creams

28 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Kerala health department is reviewing a medical officer’s demand to bar doctors from recommending cosmetic creams that lack statutory approval, and to penalise any practitioner who violates the Drugs and Cosmetics Act.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Regulatory bodies, health governance); GS Paper III – Health, Nutrition & Family Welfare (Regulation of medicines & cosmetics).

Key Data & Stats: No specific quantitative data released in the news

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Cosmetic products in India are governed by the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the rules framed thereunder. Several incidents of dermatological reactions to “unapproved” skin‑lightening or anti‑aging creams have prompted calls for stricter enforcement, especially when doctors endorse such products.
  • Significance: The move underscores the state’s role in safeguarding public health, reinforces the principle of “right to health” under Article 21 of the Constitution, and tests the efficacy of regulatory mechanisms (CDSCO, State FDA, Medical Council) in curbing unsafe medical‑cosmetic cross‑prescriptions.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Over‑lap of jurisdiction between State Health Department and Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO).
    • Lack of a clear statutory provision expressly prohibiting doctors from recommending non‑approved cosmetics.
    • Enforcement bottlenecks: monitoring prescriptions, tracing supply‑chain, and imposing penalties.
    • Potential conflict with professional autonomy and freedom of speech.
  • Way Forward: • Amend the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules to include a specific clause restricting medical practitioners from endorsing non‑approved cosmetics.
    • Empower State Medical Councils to discipline errant doctors under their code of ethics.
    • Set up a joint monitoring cell of State FDA and Health Department for periodic audits of prescriptions.
    • Launch consumer‑awareness campaigns under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, highlighting risks of unapproved creams.
    • Consider a committee (e.g., a sub‑committee of the ARC on Health) to review and recommend a model guideline for doctor‑cosmetic interactions.

Prelims Pointers

  • Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 – regulates cosmetics and medicines.
  • Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) – statutory body granting product approvals.
  • Article 21 of the Constitution – right to health as part of the right to life.
  • Consumer Protection Act, 2019 – provides redressal for unsafe consumer goods.
