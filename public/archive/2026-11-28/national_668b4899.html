Delimitation Bill LIVE: 'The number 850 represents murder of democracy,' says Rahul; DMK announces support for Centre's proposal

28 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Union Cabinet introduced the Delimitation Bill to redraw parliamentary and assembly constituencies based on the latest census. Rahul Gandhi criticised the proposal, branding the figure 850 as a “murder of democracy,” while the DMK announced its support for the Centre’s initiative.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Delimitation Commission, Article 82, 84th Amendment); GS Paper I – Indian Society & Social Justice (representation of SC/ST, demographic shifts).

Key Data & Stats: Article 82 (delimitation of constituencies); 84th Amendment (freeze on seat increase till 2026); 850 (Rahul Gandhi’s cited figure for total seats post‑delimitation).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The last delimitation exercise was carried out in 2002‑08 based on the 2001 Census. The 84th Amendment (2001) froze the increase in Lok Sabha seats until after the 2026 Census, citing concerns over population‑based representation. The present Bill seeks to lift that freeze and redraw boundaries using the 2021 Census data.
  • Significance: Redrawing constituencies will affect the balance of power among states, alter the number of seats reserved for SC/ST, and potentially reshape electoral fortunes. It also tests the federal principle by addressing demographic shifts that have long‑term implications for resource allocation and political representation.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Political opposition argues that the proposed increase to 850 seats could dilute the value of each vote and favour larger states. • Legal questions arise on whether the Bill complies with the constitutional freeze under the 84th Amendment. • Lack of a transparent, time‑bound methodology may invite accusations of gerrymandering.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute an independent Delimitation Commission chaired by a retired Supreme Court judge, with representation from the Election Commission and the Ministry of Law. • Seek advisory input from the ARC on the impact of seat re‑allocation on fiscal federalism. • Ensure judicial review mechanisms are in place to address any constitutional challenges promptly.

Prelims Pointers

  • Constitutional provision – Article 82 (delimitation of constituencies).
  • 84th Amendment (2001) – freeze on increase in Lok Sabha seats till after 2026 Census.
  • Delimitation Commission – a constitutional body empowered to redraw constituency boundaries.
  • Current Lok Sabha strength – 543 seats; proposed increase to 850 seats (as cited by opposition).
