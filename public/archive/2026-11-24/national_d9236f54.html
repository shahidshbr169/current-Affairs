CAQM report in Supreme Court shows huge gap between policy and groundwork in curbing vehicular emissions

24 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Supreme Court‑monitored CAQM report highlights a stark implementation gap in India’s EV push – despite 12.94 lakh electric vehicles registered in Delhi and the three NCR states, only 16,785 public charging points exist, underscoring inadequate infrastructure to meet policy targets.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Institutions, Policies & Governance; GS Paper III – Environment, Climate Change & Sustainable Development; GS Paper I – Indian Polity & Constitution (Article 48A, Article 246).

Key Data & Stats: 12.94 lakh EVs (Delhi + NCR), 16,785 charging points, 2024 (CAQM report in SC)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The National Electric Mobility Mission Plan (NEMMP) 2020 and the FAME‑India Scheme aim for 30% EV penetration by 2030. CAQM, a statutory body under the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways, was directed by the Supreme Court to assess on‑ground implementation.
  • Significance: Adequate charging infrastructure is critical for achieving emission‑reduction targets under the Paris Agreement and for reducing urban air pollution, a key public‑health concern in the NCR region.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Severe infrastructure deficit (≈1 charging point per 77 EVs).
    • Funding bottlenecks for private players; lack of clear PPP model.
    • Regulatory ambiguity on land allocation, grid capacity, and standards for fast chargers.
    • Potential legal tussles over compliance with the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2020.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute a high‑level inter‑ministerial committee (e.g., under the ARC) to devise a phased rollout of 1 charging point per 10 EVs by 2026.
    • Adopt a model‑bill for state‑level EV charging mandates, similar to the “Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (EV‑Charging) Bill”.
    • Incentivise private investment through viability gap funding and tax rebates.
    • Align charging standards with the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) to ensure interoperability.

Prelims Pointers

  • CAQM – Central Authority for Quality Management (statutory body under MoRTH).
  • Supreme Court’s role in monitoring implementation of environmental policies.
  • FAME‑India Scheme & National Electric Mobility Mission Plan (NEMMP) 2020.
  • Charging infrastructure ratio: 16,785 points for 12.94 lakh EVs (≈1:77).
