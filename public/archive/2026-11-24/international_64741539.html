Brazil, India are building strategic ties for a changing world, says Brazil’s Foreign Minister

24 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: Brazil and India are deepening strategic cooperation, with both sides open to using local currencies for a portion of bilateral trade, reflecting a broader trend of de‑linking from the U.S. dollar in international transactions.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; International Economic Relations; Role of BRICS and other multilateral forums.

Key Data & Agreements: use of local currencies for bilateral trade

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The move signals a shift towards a multipolar financial architecture, reducing reliance on the U.S. dollar and enhancing the strategic autonomy of emerging economies within BRICS and beyond.
  • Impact on India: Facilitates smoother trade with Brazil, lowers forex conversion costs, and strengthens India’s leverage in negotiating similar arrangements with other BRICS members and Global South partners.
  • Way Forward: India should formalise a currency‑swap mechanism with Brazil, explore a broader BRICS currency framework, and coordinate with the RBI to develop hedging tools for businesses.

Prelims Pointers

  • BRICS – Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa – a platform for financial cooperation beyond the dollar.
  • India–Brazil trade in FY 2024‑25 crossed US$ 5 billion, with key sectors: pharmaceuticals, agro‑products, and IT services.
  • Currency‑swap agreements are already in place between India and several BRICS members (e.g., Russia, UAE).
  • Global trend: Over 30 countries have signed bilateral agreements to settle trade in local currencies since 2020.
