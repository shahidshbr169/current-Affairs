Vakiti Srihari releases brochure on Khelo India Youth Games

18 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Vakiti Srihari, a senior official of the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports, unveiled the official brochure for the upcoming Khelo India Youth Games (KIYG). The brochure outlines the event’s schedule, participating states/UTs, and the government’s objectives of talent identification and grassroots sports development.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper I – Sports, Youth Affairs & Sports Policy; GS Paper II – Governance, Policy Implementation, Role of Statutory Bodies (Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports, Sports Authority of India)

Key Data & Stats: Khelo India Youth Games launched in 2018 Participation from 37 states & UTs Over 10,000 young athletes (U‑17 & U‑21) expected Budget allocation FY 2024‑25: ₹1,200 crore

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: KIYG is a flagship initiative of the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports aimed at creating a robust pipeline of sporting talent from the grassroots. It succeeds the earlier Khelo India Games (2018‑2022) and aligns with the National Sports Policy 2019.
  • Significance: The event serves as a talent‑spotting platform for national and international competitions, promotes inclusive sports participation across gender and regions, and helps achieve the government’s goal of increasing India’s medal tally at the Olympics.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Persistent issues include inadequate sports infrastructure in remote districts, uneven funding across states, and limited post‑event support for identified talent. Coordination between the Ministry, Sports Authority of India (SAI), and state sports councils often faces bureaucratic delays.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen the “Sports Infrastructure Development Fund” under the Ministry for tier‑2/3 districts.
    • Institutionalise a “Talent Nurturing Cell” under SAI to provide scholarships, coaching, and exposure to identified athletes.
    • Periodic audit by the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) to ensure transparent fund utilisation.
    • Encourage public‑private partnerships (PPP) for building and maintaining training centres, guided by recommendations of the NITI Aayog’s Sports Committee.

Prelims Pointers

  • Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports – responsible for KIYG implementation.
  • Sports Authority of India (SAI) – nodal agency for talent identification and training.
  • National Sports Policy 2019 – provides the policy framework for KIYG.
  • Constitutional provision: Article 45 (Directive Principles) encourages the State to raise the level of nutrition and standard of living, interpreted to include promotion of physical fitness and sports.
