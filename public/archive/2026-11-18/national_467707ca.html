Extended market hours not a priority for India, says Sebi Chairman

18 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey stated that extending stock‑market trading hours is not a priority for India, arguing that longer sessions may not enhance market efficiency and could heighten cybersecurity risks, strain liquidity and increase operational costs for participants.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Indian Economy & Markets; GS Paper II – Institutions & Governance (Regulatory bodies, Securities and Exchange Board of India)

Key Data & Stats: SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Over the past few years, several global exchanges (e.g., NYSE, NASDAQ) have experimented with extended or 24‑hour trading to capture cross‑border flows. In India, periodic proposals by market participants to lengthen the BSE/NSE session have been tabled, prompting SEBI to review the impact on market micro‑structure.
  • Significance: Trading‑hour decisions affect market depth, price discovery, and investor protection. A longer session could attract foreign institutional investors operating in different time zones, but may also expose the market to heightened cyber‑threats and dilute liquidity during off‑peak periods, thereby influencing overall market efficiency.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • **Cybersecurity:** Prolonged connectivity expands the attack surface for hackers, demanding robust real‑time monitoring and higher IT expenditure.
    • **Liquidity:** Empirical studies show that liquidity concentrates during core Indian market hours; extending hours may lead to thin order books and volatile price swings.
    • **Cost Implications:** Brokers, clearing corporations and depositories would need to upgrade infrastructure and staff schedules, raising transaction costs for traders.
    • **Regulatory Oversight:** Continuous surveillance by SEBI and market‑wide risk‑management mechanisms would need scaling, straining existing resources.
  • Way Forward:
    • Commission a focused study under the **SEBI‑appointed Committee on Market Micro‑Structure** to quantify liquidity patterns and cyber‑risk exposure.
    • Adopt a phased pilot of extended hours for a limited set of securities (e.g., large‑cap equities) with real‑time cyber‑security audits.
    • Align any extension with the **National Cyber Security Policy, 2023** and ensure mandatory compliance with ISO/IEC 27001 standards for market participants.
    • Consider a **Sarkaria‑type inter‑agency coordination mechanism** involving the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Electronics & IT, and the Reserve Bank of India to harmonise regulatory, technological and monetary implications.

Prelims Pointers

  • SEBI – statutory regulator of securities market (established under SEBI Act, 1992).
  • Trading‑hour extension debates relate to market efficiency, liquidity, and cyber‑security.
  • Key concerns: Cybersecurity risk, Liquidity dilution, Higher operational costs.
  • Relevant constitutional provision: Article 246 (legislative competence) – SEBI functions under Union List (finance & trade).
