Initiate action against persons who threw stones at elephant in Nagarahole, says Forest Minister

18 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: The Karnataka Forest Minister, Ramalinga Reddy, has ordered forest officials to take stringent action against individuals who threw stones at an elephant inside the Nagarahole forest limits, underscoring the state’s commitment to wildlife protection and deterrence of human‑wildlife conflict.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment, Ecology & Biodiversity; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Wildlife protection laws & administrative mechanisms)

Key Data & Stats: 2024 (year of incident) | Nagarahole Tiger Reserve, Karnataka

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Nagarahole, part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, hosts a significant elephant population. Incidents of human‑wildlife conflict have risen with expanding tourism and encroachment, prompting stricter enforcement of the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.
  • Significance: Prompt action signals zero tolerance for harassment of protected species, reinforces compliance with the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, and helps maintain ecological balance, tourism revenue, and community safety.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Identifying perpetrators in dense forest terrain, ensuring due process, and balancing livelihood concerns of local communities who may feel threatened by wildlife. There is also a need for systematic monitoring to prevent repeat offences.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen on‑ground patrolling and install CCTV/thermal cameras at conflict hotspots.
    • Empower the State Wildlife Board to fast‑track penalties under the Act.
    • Initiate community‑based awareness programmes on co‑existence.
    • Consider a review by the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL) for additional protective measures in Nagarahole.

Prelims Pointers

  • Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 – provisions for protection of elephants and penalties for harassment.
  • Article 48A of the Constitution – State’s duty to protect and improve the environment.
  • State Forest Department & Karnataka Forest Minister – administrative authority for enforcement.
  • Nagarahole Tiger Reserve – part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, home to ~200 elephants.
