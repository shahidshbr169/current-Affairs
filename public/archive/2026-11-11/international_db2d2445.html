IBM to end U.S. visa, green card sponsorship for most roles in its Consulting arm

11 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: IBM will cease sponsoring U.S. work visas and green cards for the majority of roles in its consulting division. Affected employees must either relocate to IBM offices outside the United States or exit the company by end‑2024.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – International Relations: Migration, diaspora and skill‑based mobility; GS Paper III – Economic Development: Foreign direct investment and MNC operations.

Key Data & Agreements: No new H‑1B/green‑card sponsorships for consulting roles | Impact period: 2024‑2025

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The decision mirrors a broader U.S. shift toward tighter immigration controls and a “re‑shoring” of high‑skill talent, influencing global talent competition among tech and consulting firms.
  • Impact on India: Indian IT professionals employed by IBM in the U.S. may lose visa pathways, prompting a potential outflow of expertise back to Indian offices or other markets; simultaneously, it could boost employment in IBM’s Indian delivery centres and increase reliance on the Indian diaspora for client engagements.
  • Way Forward: India should (i) engage with the U.S. on reciprocal skilled‑worker visa arrangements, (ii) strengthen domestic up‑skilling programmes to absorb displaced talent, and (iii) leverage the situation to negotiate greater participation of Indian IT firms in U.S. government digital‑transformation contracts.

Prelims Pointers

  • U.S. immigration policy tightening affects H‑1B and employment‑based green‑card categories.
  • IBM’s move is part of a trend where MNCs reassess visa‑dependent staffing models.
  • Potential implications for India’s services export earnings and the size of the Indian IT diaspora in the United States.
  • Relevance to Quad’s digital cooperation agenda, where skilled mobility is a key discussion point.
