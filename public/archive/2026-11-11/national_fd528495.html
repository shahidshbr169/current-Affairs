Cabinet approves fertiliser subsidy of Rs 62,370 crore for rabi season

11 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Union Cabinet has sanctioned a fertiliser subsidy of Rs 62,370 crore for the upcoming Rabi season, aiming to curb input cost inflation for farmers. In the same meeting, the Cabinet extended the Stand‑Up India Scheme and the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units for a further five years up to March 2031, reinforcing credit support for MSMEs.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Economic Development, Agriculture, MSME sector.

Key Data & Stats: Rs 62,370 crore fertiliser subsidy; 5 years extension of Stand‑Up India & Credit Guarantee Fund; March 2031 end‑date; Rabi 2024‑25 season.

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Fertiliser subsidies have been a core component of India’s agricultural policy since the 1970s, aimed at ensuring affordable inputs and food security. Recent global commodity price spikes and domestic inflation prompted a fresh allocation. The Stand‑Up India Scheme (launched 2016) and the Credit Guarantee Fund (CGFMU, 2020) were introduced to improve MSME access to finance.
  • Significance: The subsidy directly reduces production costs, potentially stabilising Rabi crop yields and curbing food‑price inflation, a key concern for the Union Budget. Extending credit support mechanisms sustains MSME growth, job creation, and aligns with the “Atmanirbhar Bharat” agenda.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Fiscal burden: The subsidy adds to the fiscal deficit, raising questions on sustainability.
    • Leakage & targeting: Past audits (CAG) have flagged diversion of subsidised fertiliser to non‑eligible users.
    • Legal/ethical: Potential conflict with WTO’s Agreement on Agriculture if subsidies are deemed trade‑distorting.
    • MSME credit: CGFMU’s claim ratio and claim settlement delays have limited its effectiveness.
  • Way Forward:
    • Adopt a “direct benefit transfer” (DBT) model for fertiliser subsidy to minimise leakage (as recommended by the NITI Aayog task‑force).
    • Periodic review of subsidy quantum linked to international fertiliser price indices.
    • Strengthen CGFMU’s claim‑settlement mechanism and raise its claim‑to‑guarantee ratio to 75 % (as per RBI’s suggestion).
    • Constitute a joint committee of the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Agriculture, and the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) to monitor implementation.

Prelims Pointers

  • Fertiliser subsidy – major component of agricultural price support; linked to food‑security policy.
  • Stand‑Up India Scheme – targets SC/ST and women entrepreneurs for bank loans ≥ ₹10 lakh.
  • Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU) – provides credit guarantee up to ₹5 crore per MSME.
  • Cabinet approval – signifies executive’s fiscal commitment; relevant to Article 112 (Finance Bill) and the role of the Union Cabinet under Article 75(1).
