Nvidia servers still in their boxes: As Jensen Huang's controversy grows, here's what a US official found inside Megaspeed's Malaysian data centre in 2024

11 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: A U.S. official discovered that a Malaysian data centre operated by Singapore‑based Megaspeed housed unopened Nvidia AI servers in 2024, raising alarms over alleged Chinese espionage linked to an executive named Alice Huang. The incident spotlights gaps in India’s oversight of foreign AI infrastructure and the strategic implications of high‑value chip imports.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Security, Law & International Relations (strategic implications, cyber‑security, export control); GS Paper II – Governance & Policy (regulatory framework, Ministry of Electronics & IT, Department of Telecommunications).

Key Data & Stats: $4.8 billion (Nvidia chips purchased by Megaspeed in the last two years) | 2024 (year of the discovery) | Malaysia (location of the data centre) | Singapore‑based (origin of Megaspeed) | Alice Huang (alleged Chinese spy).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Post‑2020, the U.S. tightened export controls on advanced AI chips (Export Control Order, 2020) to curb technology transfer to adversarial states. India’s rapid AI push has attracted foreign AI‑infrastructure firms, but regulatory oversight of foreign‑owned data centres remains fragmented.
  • Significance: Unused high‑performance Nvidia servers could be repurposed for data harvesting or AI model training, posing a strategic security risk. The episode underscores the need for robust vetting of foreign AI investments and aligns with concerns about “technology leakage” to China.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Absence of a dedicated “AI‑Infrastructure Clearance” mechanism; existing approvals are under the Ministry of Electronics & IT and the Department of Telecommunications, which lack AI‑specific expertise.
    • Legal ambiguity in applying the Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act to AI‑hardware.
    • Coordination gaps between intelligence agencies and economic ministries.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute an inter‑ministerial “Strategic AI Infrastructure Committee” (ARC‑type) chaired by the Prime Minister’s Office to review foreign AI‑hardware investments.
    • Amend the Export Control Order to include AI‑accelerators above a defined compute threshold.
    • Mandate real‑time reporting of AI‑related capital imports > $100 million to the Ministry of Commerce.
    • Strengthen cyber‑surveillance of foreign data centres under the National Cyber Security Policy, 2023.

Prelims Pointers

  • Export Control Order, 2020 – regulates export of advanced computing chips.
  • Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992 – legal basis for restricting strategic imports.
  • Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology (MeitY) – nodal agency for AI‑hardware approvals.
  • Strategic AI Infrastructure Committee – recommended inter‑ministerial body (ARC‑style) for oversight.
