U.S. and China should stay clear-eyed on AI cooperation and competition, says Scott Bessent

11 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: U.S. Treasury official Scott Bessent, ahead of President Donald Trump’s third summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping, emphasized the need for a balanced, reciprocal approach to AI cooperation and competition between the United States and China.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; International Relations – US‑China strategic rivalry and technology governance.

Key Data & Agreements: Third US‑China summit in 2024

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The statement underscores the widening AI race, where both superpowers seek leadership while avoiding outright conflict, shaping norms for emerging technologies in multilateral fora such as the G20 and UN.
  • Impact on India: India must navigate a delicate balance—leveraging AI collaborations with both Washington and Beijing, safeguarding its own tech ecosystem, and preventing a bifurcated global AI architecture that could limit Indian startups.
  • Way Forward: India should pursue a “strategic autonomy” policy: (i) engage in multilateral AI governance initiatives; (ii) deepen Indo‑US AI research ties under existing frameworks (e.g., US‑India Strategic Partnership); (iii) negotiate bilateral AI data‑sharing protocols with China while protecting critical infrastructure.

Prelims Pointers

  • US‑China AI cooperation/competition is a key agenda item in the upcoming summit.
  • Scott Bessent is the senior US Treasury official leading the economic dialogue.
  • The meeting marks the third high‑level US‑China summit in 2024, reflecting intensified diplomatic engagement.
  • India’s AI strategy (National AI Strategy, 2023) emphasizes participation in global governance while maintaining data sovereignty.
