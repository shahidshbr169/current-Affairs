Defence Ministry inks ₹498 crore INS Betwa refit contract

11 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Ministry of Defence has awarded a ₹498 crore contract to refit the Brahmaputra‑class frigate INS Betwa. The refit will replace its ageing steam‑based propulsion with a modern diesel‑engine system, enhancing operational efficiency and extending the vessel’s service life.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Defence, Technology & Industry; GS Paper II – Governance & Policy (Defence procurement, Make in India)

Key Data & Stats: ₹498 crore, INS Betwa, Brahmaputra‑class frigate, Steam‑based propulsion → Diesel‑engine propulsion

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: India’s naval fleet faces a capability gap due to ageing platforms and limited indigenous propulsion technology. Earlier, the Ministry launched the “Strategic Partnership” model and the Defence Procurement Procedure (DPP) 2020 to modernise the fleet and reduce reliance on foreign technology.
  • Significance: Upgrading propulsion improves fuel efficiency, reduces maintenance downtime, and aligns with the “Make in India” agenda by encouraging domestic engine manufacturing. It also extends the operational lifespan of INS Betwa, contributing to maritime security in the Indian Ocean Region.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Integration risk – retrofitting diesel engines into a hull designed for steam may pose engineering and certification hurdles.
    • Cost‑benefit – the high outlay must be justified against acquiring new vessels under the Indigenous Sub‑marines and Warships (ISW) programme.
    • Procurement transparency – ensuring the contract adheres to DPP norms to avoid delays and cost overruns.
  • Way Forward: • Conduct a post‑refit performance audit to benchmark fuel savings and operational readiness.
    • Form a joint technical committee (including DRDO, Indian Navy, and private OEMs) to standardise diesel‑engine retrofits for other legacy ships.
    • Align future refits with the “Naval Shipbuilding Programme” and consider a phased de‑commissioning plan for steam‑propelled vessels.

Prelims Pointers

  • Defence Procurement Procedure (DPP) 2020 – framework governing defence contracts.
  • Make in India – policy driving indigenous defence manufacturing.
  • INS Betwa – Brahmaputra‑class frigate commissioned in 2004, now undergoing propulsion upgrade.
  • Steam vs. diesel propulsion – impact on fuel efficiency, maintenance and operational range.
