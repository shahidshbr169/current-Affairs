What the Punjab and Haryana HC said about protecting LGBTQIA+ students in schools? | Explained

11 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Punjab and Haryana High Court directed all schools to implement the Supreme Court’s Sukdeb Saha guidelines for safeguarding LGBTQIA+ students, after a petition by the mother of a 16‑year‑old who allegedly faced bullying, harassment and sexual assault and died by suicide in 2022. The order underscores the judiciary’s role in enforcing inclusive education and student‑safety norms.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Social Justice, Human Rights, Education.

Key Data & Stats: 2020 – Sukdeb Saha Supreme Court Guidelines, 2022 – Suicide of 16‑year‑old student

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Supreme Court, in Sukdeb Saha vs. Union of India (2020), issued comprehensive guidelines for schools to create a safe, non‑discriminatory environment for LGBTQIA+ children, covering anti‑bullying policies, sensitisation of staff, and grievance redressal mechanisms. A petition filed by a grieving mother alleged that the school failed to follow these norms, leading to the tragic death of her child.
  • Significance: The High Court’s directive reinforces the constitutional guarantees of Article 14 (Equality) and Article 21 (Right to Life & Personal Liberty) in the educational context. It also operationalises the Supreme Court’s pronouncement, setting a precedent for other states to monitor compliance with LGBTQIA+ protection norms in schools.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Implementation gaps persist due to lack of teacher training, inadequate monitoring mechanisms, and societal stigma. Schools often lack clear SOPs for reporting sexual harassment, and existing grievance cells may be understaffed. Moreover, the absence of a dedicated statutory body to audit compliance creates a legal‑administrative vacuum.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute a state‑level “LGBTQIA+ Education Monitoring Committee” (modelled on the National Commission for Protection of Child Rights) to audit school compliance annually.
    • Amend the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act (RTE) to incorporate explicit provisions on LGBTQIA+ safety, referencing the Sukdeb Saha guidelines.
    • Launch mandatory sensitisation modules for teachers under the National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT) curriculum.
    • Encourage the Supreme Court‑appointed Committee on Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POSCO) to issue a specific advisory on school‑based bullying of LGBTQIA+ students.

Prelims Pointers

  • Supreme Court’s Sukdeb Saha vs. Union of India (2020) – guidelines for LGBTQIA+ student protection.
  • Constitutional provisions: Article 14 (Equality), Article 21 (Right to Life & Personal Liberty).
  • Relevant statutes: Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act (RTE), Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act.
  • Judicial oversight: High Court’s role in enforcing Supreme Court directives at the state level.
