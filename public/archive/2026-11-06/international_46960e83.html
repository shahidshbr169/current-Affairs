Mahabharata, Arthashastra, Rajatarangini manuscripts added to UNESCO memory regional register

6 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: UNESCO’s Memory of the World Regional Register has inscribed three ancient Indian manuscripts – the Mahabharata, Arthashastra and Rajatarangini – recognizing their unparalleled documentary and civilisational value. Union Culture Minister Gajendra Singh Shekhawat hailed the move as a landmark affirmation of India’s rich heritage on the global stage.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – International organisations (UNESCO), cultural diplomacy, soft power and India’s participation in global heritage conventions.

Key Data & Agreements: UNESCO Memory of the World (Regional) – 2024 inscription

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The inclusion bolsters India’s cultural soft power, enhancing its standing in UNESCO and reinforcing the narrative of a continuous civilisational legacy that can be leveraged in multilateral cultural forums.
  • Impact on India: Directly augments India’s cultural diplomacy toolkit, providing a credible platform for bilateral cultural exchanges and tourism promotion; indirectly it strengthens India’s claim in heritage‑related negotiations (e.g., repatriation of artifacts, UNESCO funding for conservation).
  • Way Forward: • Institutionalise a dedicated “UNESCO Heritage Liaison Cell” within the Ministry of Culture to monitor and propose future nominations.
    • Mobilise academic and civil‑society partners for conservation, digitisation and public outreach.
    • Use the inscription as a springboard for cultural‑tourism corridors linking the manuscript sites with international visitor circuits.

Prelims Pointers

  • UNESCO’s Memory of the World Programme aims to safeguard documentary heritage of universal value.
  • Manuscripts inscribed: Mahabharata (epic), Arthashastra (political economy treatise), Rajatarangini (Kashmiri chronicle).
  • India’s cultural soft‑power leverages UNESCO platforms to project its civilisational narrative globally.
  • Potential for increased heritage tourism and international research collaborations.
