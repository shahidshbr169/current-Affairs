TVK MLA’s ‘horse trading’ claim: Case registered against former Madurai Deputy Mayor P.M. Mannan

6 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Madurai Central TVK MLA Mustafa lodged a complaint alleging that former Deputy Mayor P.M. Mannan engaged in “horse‑trading” to influence the MLA’s election. The Central Crime Branch (CCB) registered a case under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 and sections relating to criminal conspiracy, and Mannan, now a member of AIADMK, has been summoned for inquiry.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (election ethics, anti‑defection, statutory bodies); GS Paper III – Security & Law (Prevention of Corruption Act, criminal conspiracy).

Key Data & Stats: Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 | Criminal Conspiracy (IPC Sec.120) | Central Crime Branch (CCB)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: “Horse‑trading” refers to the illicit purchase of votes or political support, undermining the sanctity of the electoral process. The Tenth Schedule (Anti‑Defection Law) and the Representation of the People Act aim to curb such practices, but enforcement often hinges on investigative agencies like the CCB.
  • Significance: The case highlights systemic vulnerabilities in electoral integrity and the potential misuse of public office for partisan gains. It also tests the effectiveness of anti‑corruption legislation in curbing political patronage.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Investigations are frequently hampered by political interference, delayed prosecutions, and limited powers of the CCB to compel testimony from elected representatives. The overlap between criminal law and political conduct raises questions about jurisdiction and the need for a specialised fast‑track mechanism.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen the autonomy and resources of investigative agencies (e.g., CCB, ED). • Introduce a statutory “Electoral Integrity Cell” with powers to fast‑track horse‑trading cases. • Periodic audit of elected officials’ assets by the Lokpal. • Consider a committee (e.g., an ARC‑type panel) to review and recommend amendments to the Representation of the People Act for stricter penalties.
