5,295 special children screened across Keralam under Anbu project

6 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Kerala’s Anbu project screened 5,295 special‑needs children, providing free follow‑up treatment, corrective eyeglasses and cataract surgeries. The initiative underscores the state’s focus on inclusive health care for vulnerable children.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Health, Social Justice & Welfare; GS Paper II – Governance & Administration (implementation of state health schemes)

Key Data & Stats: 5,295 children screened; free corrective eyeglasses and cataract surgeries provided; statewide rollout under the Anbu project.

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Kerala has a long‑standing emphasis on public health and inclusive education. The Anbu project was launched to address visual impairments and other health issues among children with special needs, aligning with the National Health Policy’s focus on preventive care.
  • Significance: Early detection of visual problems prevents long‑term disability, improves learning outcomes, and reduces future health‑care costs. The scheme also operationalises the Right to Education (RTE) mandate of providing a conducive environment for all children.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Identifying eligible children in remote areas, ensuring continuity of post‑screening care, and sustaining funding for free spectacles and surgeries are key bottlenecks. Coordination between health and education departments can be fragmented, and data‑management systems need strengthening.
  • Way Forward: • Institutionalise school‑based vision screening under the State Health Mission.
    • Create a joint task‑force of the Health Department, Education Department and NITI Aayog for periodic review.
    • Leverage the recommendations of the ARC on Child Health to expand coverage and integrate tele‑ophthalmology.
    • Mobilise corporate social responsibility (CSR) funds for long‑term financing of spectacles and surgical kits.

Prelims Pointers

  • Kerala’s Anbu project – state‑level health initiative for special‑needs children.
  • Right to Education Act, Article 21A – obligation to provide inclusive education.
  • National Health Policy 2017 – emphasis on preventive health care and universal eye health.
  • State Health Mission – framework for implementing school‑based health programmes.
