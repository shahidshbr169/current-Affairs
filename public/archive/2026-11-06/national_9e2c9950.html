Social media companies must act proactively on child safety, grievances, algorithmic transparency: Vaishnaw

6 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw urged social‑media platforms to adopt proactive measures for child safety, address user grievances promptly and ensure algorithmic transparency. He emphasized the need for a robust, direct grievance‑redressal mechanism to protect vulnerable users and build trust in digital ecosystems.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Technology, Governance & Digital Initiatives; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution & Polity (Regulatory frameworks & statutory bodies)

Key Data & Stats: IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw Child safety on digital platforms Algorithmic transparency

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Rising incidents of online harassment, exposure to harmful content and lack of transparency in content recommendation engines have prompted calls for stricter regulation. Existing provisions under the IT Act and the pending Data Protection Bill offer limited safeguards for minors.
  • Significance: Protecting children online safeguards fundamental rights under Article 21 (right to life & liberty) and aligns with India’s commitments under the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child. A transparent grievance mechanism enhances digital trust and supports the Digital India agenda.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Jurisdictional complexities with global platforms
    • Balancing content moderation with freedom of expression (Article 19)
    • Technical feasibility of real‑time algorithm audits
    • Potential conflict with Section 79 safe‑harbour provisions of the IT Act
  • Way Forward: • Amend the IT Act to mandate a statutory grievance‑redressal cell for social media users.
    • Constitute an independent oversight committee (modeled on the ARC) to audit algorithms for bias and child‑safety compliance.
    • Align platform obligations with the forthcoming Data Protection Bill and the National Digital Communications Policy.
    • Encourage self‑regulatory codes of practice, with periodic reporting to the Ministry of Electronics & IT and the NCPCR.

Prelims Pointers

  • Section 79 of the IT Act – safe‑harbour protection for intermediaries.
  • National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) – statutory body overseeing child safety online.
  • Algorithmic transparency – emerging regulatory focus under the Data Protection Bill.
  • Proposed grievance‑redressal mechanism – to be framed under the Digital Services Regulation framework.
