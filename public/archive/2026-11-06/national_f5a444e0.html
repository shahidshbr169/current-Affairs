Keralam’s mangrove cover expanded in past decade despite prior decline, finds study

6 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: A study by ICAR‑CMFRI using satellite imagery from 2017 and 2026 shows that Kerala’s mangrove cover has increased over the last decade, reversing an earlier period of decline. The expansion underscores the effectiveness of recent conservation measures and highlights mangroves’ role in coastal resilience.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment, Ecology, Biodiversity & Climate Change; GS Paper II – Governance (policy implementation & statutory bodies)

Key Data & Stats: 2017 (baseline satellite image) – 2026 (latest satellite image); “past decade” of net increase in mangrove area (exact figures not disclosed).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Kerala’s coastal districts historically faced mangrove loss due to urbanisation, aquaculture, and illegal encroachments. The National Mangrove Conservation Programme (NMCP) and state‑level afforestation drives were launched to restore these ecosystems.
  • Significance: Mangroves act as natural buffers against storm surges, sequester carbon, support fisheries, and preserve biodiversity. Their expansion directly contributes to climate‑change mitigation and coastal livelihood security.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Persistent threats include land‑use change, pollution, and inadequate enforcement of coastal regulation zones. Remote‑sensing data must be integrated with ground‑level monitoring to prevent “paper‑based” successes.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen NMCP implementation with periodic satellite audits.
    • Involve local communities and fisherfolk in mangrove stewardship (co‑management models).
    • Establish an inter‑agency task‑force (MoEFCC, Kerala Forest Department, ICAR‑CMFRI) modeled on the ARC for ecosystem‑based adaptation.
    • Link mangrove restoration to climate‑finance mechanisms (e.g., Green Climate Fund) and SDG targets.

Prelims Pointers

  • ICAR‑Central Marine Fisheries Research Institute (CMFRI) – apex body for marine research.
  • National Mangrove Conservation Programme (NMCP) under Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.
  • Role of mangroves in carbon sequestration and coastal protection – relevant to SDG 13 (Climate Action) & SDG 14 (Life Below Water).
  • Use of satellite remote sensing for ecological monitoring – a key tool for evidence‑based policy.
