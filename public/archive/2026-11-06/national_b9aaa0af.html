Congress says people must express outrage over inflation by teaching BJP a lesson in upcoming polls

6 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: The Congress has urged citizens to voice their anger over the persistent rise in inflation and household debt, framing the upcoming elections as a referendum on the Modi government's economic management.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Economic Growth, Inflation & Debt Management; GS Paper II – Governance, Policy Implementation, Fiscal Federalism

Key Data & Stats: N/A

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Since 2022, India has grappled with a “dual vicious cycle” of high consumer price inflation and rising household indebtedness, driven by global commodity price shocks, supply‑chain disruptions, and expansive fiscal stimulus.
  • Significance: Persistent inflation erodes real wages and savings, while mounting debt limits consumption, affecting electoral behaviour and prompting calls for policy accountability.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Balancing price stability with growth; limited fiscal space for direct subsidies; RBI’s mandate to curb inflation versus political pressure for short‑term relief; regional disparities in debt burden.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen RBI’s operational independence (Article 246 (2) – Union List: Finance).
    • Deploy targeted, time‑bound subsidies under the Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) framework to cushion vulnerable groups.
    • Conduct a periodic review of the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act targets through the Economic Advisory Council (EAC).
    • Encourage state‑center coordination via the Finance Commission to address regional debt stress.

Prelims Pointers

  • Consumer Price Index (CPI) – primary measure of inflation.
  • Fiscal deficit and debt‑to‑GDP ratio – key indicators of fiscal health.
  • Reserve Bank of India’s monetary policy framework – inflation targeting at 4 ± 2%.
  • Article 246 (2) of the Constitution – Union’s exclusive power to legislate on finance.
