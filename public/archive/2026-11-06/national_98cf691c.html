APSCHE moots major overhaul of PG education in 28 State universities

6 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Andhra Pradesh State Council of Higher Education (APSCHE) has proposed a sweeping reform of postgraduate (PG) education across 28 state universities. The centerpiece of the reform is a uniform credit structure of 80‑88 credits for two‑year PG programmes, aimed at standardising curricula and enhancing academic mobility.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Education, Skill Development & Technical Progress; GS Paper II – Governance & Public Policy (higher‑education reforms)

Key Data & Stats: 28 state universities | 80‑88 credits per programme | 2‑year duration

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Existing PG programmes in Andhra Pradesh exhibit disparate credit requirements and curriculum designs, leading to confusion for students and employers. The UGC’s National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 advocates a credit‑based, outcome‑oriented system, prompting APSCHE to align state institutions with national standards.
  • Significance: A common credit framework will facilitate inter‑university credit transfer, improve comparability of qualifications, and boost employability. It also supports the broader goal of creating a “single window” higher‑education ecosystem, essential for skill‑driven growth.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Implementation may clash with university autonomy, require faculty up‑skilling, and demand robust accreditation mechanisms. Funding for curriculum redesign, digital infrastructure for credit tracking, and resistance from institutions accustomed to legacy systems are potential bottlenecks.
  • Way Forward: • Form a steering committee comprising UGC, APSCHE, and representatives of the 28 universities (akin to an ARC‑type panel).
    • Phase‑wise rollout with pilot programmes, followed by periodic audits.
    • Align the credit system with the National Credit Transfer Scheme (NCTS) under NEP 2020.
    • Provide financial assistance and capacity‑building grants to universities for curriculum redesign and faculty development.

Prelims Pointers

  • APSCHE – Andhra Pradesh State Council of Higher Education
  • UGC’s National Education Policy 2020 – emphasis on credit‑based, outcome‑oriented learning
  • Common credit framework: 80‑88 credits for 2‑year PG programmes
  • Applicable to 28 state universities in Andhra Pradesh
