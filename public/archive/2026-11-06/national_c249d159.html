Inward FDI to ODI ratio narrows to 4.2 times in FY26 from 5.5 times: RBI

6 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The RBI’s annual census shows that the gap between inward foreign direct investment (FDI) and India’s overseas direct investment (ODI) has narrowed, with the inward‑to‑outward ratio falling to 4.2 times in FY26 from 5.5 times. The United States has emerged as the single largest source of outstanding FDI in India, underscoring a shift in the investment landscape.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Economic Development, Investment & Foreign Trade; GS Paper II – Governance (Regulatory bodies – RBI, FEMA); Optional – International Relations (FDI trends & bilateral investment).

Key Data & Stats: Inward‑to‑ODI ratio: 4.2:1 (FY26), Previous ratio: 5.5:1, US: Largest source of outstanding FDI, RBI Annual Census of FDI

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The RBI publishes an annual census of foreign investment under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA). Over the past decade, India’s ODI has grown faster than inbound FDI, widening the ratio. Recent policy measures – liberalised FDI caps, Make in India, and strategic sector incentives – have boosted inbound flows, especially from the US.
  • Significance: A narrowing ratio signals improved capital account balance, greater confidence of foreign investors, and potential technology transfer. It also reduces the external debt burden associated with overseas acquisitions and aligns with the government’s goal of attracting high‑value FDI.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Persistent sectoral caps, lengthy approval processes, and repatriation restrictions can still deter investors. ODI growth raises concerns about capital outflows, especially in volatile global markets, and may affect domestic investment capacity if not monitored.
  • Way Forward: • Streamline FDI approval through a single‑window system (e.g., Invest India). • Periodic review of sectoral caps by a high‑level committee (similar to the FDI Policy Review Committee). • Strengthen RBI’s ODI monitoring framework to prevent excessive outflows. • Encourage greenfield FDI in high‑tech and renewable sectors to complement ODI.

Prelims Pointers

  • Inward FDI vs. Outward Direct Investment (ODI) ratio – a key indicator of capital account health.
  • RBI’s Annual Census of Foreign Direct Investment – statutory source of data under FEMA.
  • FY26 – fiscal year in which the ratio narrowed to 4.2 times.
  • United States – top source of outstanding FDI in India.
