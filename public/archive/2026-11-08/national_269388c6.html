Punjab anti-drug activist Jhota cremated in his native place Mansa

8 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Anti‑drug activist Jhota was shot dead in broad daylight at a medical store in the bustling market of Mansa, Punjab on 13 Oct. His cremation in his native town underscores the escalating violence against drug‑reform advocates and raises concerns over law‑and‑order and protection of civil society actors.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; Law and Justice; Public Administration; Social Justice

Key Data & Stats: 13 Oct | Mansa, Punjab | Anti‑drug activism

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Punjab has been grappling with a severe narcotics crisis for over a decade, prompting civil‑society initiatives, NGOs and individual activists like Jhota to campaign for stricter enforcement and rehabilitation. The state’s anti‑drug policies (e.g., Punjab Drug De‑Addiction Programme) have faced criticism for inadequate implementation and alleged collusion.
  • Significance: The murder of a prominent activist highlights gaps in the protection of citizens exercising their right to free speech and public interest litigation (Article 19(1)(a) & Article 21 of the Constitution). It also signals a potential climate of impunity that can deter civil participation in drug‑control efforts.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Weak policing and delayed investigation of crimes against activists.
    • Possible nexus between drug traffickers, local power structures, and law‑enforcement agencies.
    • Lack of a dedicated witness‑protection/activist‑safety mechanism under existing statutes.
    • Insufficient data‑driven monitoring of drug‑related incidents at the district level.
  • Way Forward:
    • Set up a fast‑track Special Investigation Team (SIT) under the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act to probe the killing.
    • Enact a state‑level “Activist Protection Act” modeled on the Witness Protection Scheme, ensuring security and legal aid.
    • Strengthen the Punjab State Commission for Women & Children to include a sub‑committee on drug‑related violence.
    • Periodic review by the Supreme Court under Article 32 to ensure compliance with fundamental rights.
    • Leverage existing bodies such as the National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) for granular data collection and public‑policy feedback.

Prelims Pointers

  • Article 19(1)(a) – Freedom of speech and expression; Article 21 – Right to life.
  • Punjab Drug De‑Addiction Programme – State‑level initiative to curb narcotics.
  • National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) – Provides data on drug‑related crimes.
  • Need for a Witness/Activist Protection Scheme – Legislative gap highlighted by the incident.
