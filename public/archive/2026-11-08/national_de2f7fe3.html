‘Dimagi Naxals’ instigating people against Constitution and democracy: PM Modi as Maoists eliminated

8 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Prime Minister Narendra Modi, while celebrating Diwali with CRPF personnel in Bastar, urged the nation to “identify and defeat” ideological supporters of Naxalism who have allegedly infiltrated political parties and civil organisations, framing them as a threat to the Constitution and democratic fabric.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; Internal Security; Law and Order; Centre‑State Relations

Key Data & Stats: Bastar, Chhattisgarh CRPF

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Naxalite‑Maoist insurgency, active since the late 1960s, has been concentrated in the “Red Corridor” covering parts of Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Maharashtra, Bihar, and Andhra Pradesh. The movement exploits socio‑economic deprivation, land‑rights issues, and perceived state neglect, leading to armed confrontations with security forces.
  • Significance: The insurgency challenges internal security, hampers development projects, and threatens the constitutional order by attempting to subvert democratic institutions. A perception of infiltration into mainstream parties raises concerns about the integrity of political processes and the rule of law.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Balancing hard‑line security operations with respect for human rights and civil liberties (Article 21, Article 19).
    • Addressing the root causes – chronic poverty, lack of infrastructure, and land‑dispute grievances – which security‑only approaches cannot resolve.
    • Ensuring effective coordination between central agencies (MHA, Ministry of Rural Development) and state governments under the federal structure.
    • Potential misuse of anti‑terror statutes (e.g., Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act) to curb legitimate dissent.
  • Way Forward:
    • Adopt a “development‑plus‑security” model: accelerate the Integrated Action Plan (IAP) for Naxal‑affected districts, focusing on roads, schools, healthcare, and livelihood schemes.
    • Strengthen community policing and intelligence‑sharing mechanisms involving local panchayats and civil society.
    • Periodic review by a high‑level committee (e.g., a re‑constituted “Naxal‑Affected Areas Development Committee” under the ARC) to monitor progress and recommend policy tweaks.
    • Review and, if necessary, amend legal provisions to ensure that anti‑insurgency measures do not infringe on constitutional rights, maintaining the balance between security and liberty.

Prelims Pointers

  • “Naxalism” – left‑wing extremism rooted in Maoist ideology, active in the Red Corridor.
  • Key statutory framework: Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 (UAPA) and provisions of the Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958 (AFSPA) in affected states.
  • Central agency: Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) – primary force for internal security operations against Naxals.
  • Constitutional relevance: Articles 19 (1)(a) (freedom of speech), 21 (right to life & liberty), and 32 (right to constitutional remedies) – often invoked in debates on security‑vs‑civil‑rights.
