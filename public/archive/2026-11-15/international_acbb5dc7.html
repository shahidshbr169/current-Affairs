Nigeria cautions against travel via Ethiopia's main airport due to altitude

15 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: Nigeria’s foreign ministry warned its citizens against transiting through Addis Ababa’s Bole International Airport, citing the city’s high altitude as a health risk. Six Nigerians with pre‑existing medical conditions have died this year while on layover at the Ethiopian hub.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – International Relations: Bilateral health and aviation safety issues; Regional groupings (African Union, IGAD) and India’s engagement with African transport corridors.

Key Data & Agreements: 6 deaths of Nigerian nationals in 2024; 2,355 m average altitude of Addis Ababa; no specific treaty involved.

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: Addis Ababa serves as a major air‑hub for East‑and‑Central Africa, linking Europe, Asia and the Middle East. Health‑related travel advisories can affect passenger confidence, cargo flows, and Ethiopia’s leverage in regional aviation alliances (e.g., African Airlines Association).
  • Impact on India: Indian carriers (Air India, IndiGo) and cargo operators use Bole as a transit point for African destinations. A perceived safety risk could reduce Indian passenger traffic, affect bilateral trade logistics, and necessitate diplomatic engagement with Ethiopia and Nigeria.
  • Way Forward: India should (i) issue a precautionary advisory for travelers with chronic ailments; (ii) seek bilateral dialogue with Ethiopia to strengthen medical facilities at the airport and share best‑practice protocols; (iii) explore alternative African hubs (e.g., Nairobi, Kigali) for Indian airlines; and (iv) collaborate with WHO/ICAO on altitude‑related health guidelines.
