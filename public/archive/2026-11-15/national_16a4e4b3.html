Commerce, Textiles Ministries make case for long-term continuation of export schemes

15 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Textiles have jointly sought a 3‑5 year extension of the Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products (RoDTEP) and the Remission of State and Central Taxes and Levies (RoSCTL) schemes. They have asked the Department of Revenue to undertake detailed impact analyses to address exporters’ demand for policy certainty and to safeguard export competitiveness.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Economic Development, Trade & Export Policies; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (inter‑ministerial coordination, statutory bodies)

Key Data & Stats: RoDTEP (launched 2020), RoSCTL (launched 2022), 3‑5 year extension sought, Exporters demand policy certainty

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: RoDTEP replaced the Merchandise Exports from India Scheme (MEIS) to align India’s export incentives with WTO rules. RoSCTL was introduced to provide similar remission benefits specifically for the textile sector, which faces high input‑cost differentials.
  • Significance: Continuation of these schemes is crucial for maintaining the price‑competitiveness of Indian exports, stabilising export earnings, and supporting sectors that are major foreign‑exchange earners. It also signals policy stability, which is vital for long‑term foreign investment and export‑oriented MSMEs.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Fiscal burden on the exchequer – remission of duties translates into revenue loss.
    • Ensuring compliance with WTO’s “no subsidy” provisions; periodic impact studies are required.
    • Coordination gap between Commerce, Textiles and the Department of Revenue may delay the impact assessment.
    • Potential for misuse or over‑reliance by exporters, leading to reduced incentive for cost‑reduction.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute an inter‑ministerial committee (including Finance, Commerce, Textiles, and DoR) to review the schemes annually.
    • Adopt a phased extension – initial 2‑year renewal followed by a comprehensive impact study before a longer term.
    • Link remission benefits to measurable export‑growth targets and compliance with WTO notifications.
    • Consider a sunset clause and periodic fiscal audit, as recommended by the Finance Ministry’s Committee on Export Incentives.

Prelims Pointers

  • RoDTEP – Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products (replaced MEIS, 2020).
  • RoSCTL – Remission of State and Central Taxes and Levies (textile‑specific, 2022).
  • Department of Revenue – tasked with impact analysis and fiscal assessment.
  • WTO compliance – export incentive schemes must not constitute prohibited subsidies.
