Keralam power purchase row: Opposition Leader Pinarayi Vijayan alleges irregularities in decision to procure 200 MW from VEH Global

15 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Kerala government decided to procure 200 MW of power from VEH Global at a price of ₹11 per unit without conducting a competitive bidding process or obtaining prior approval from the Kerala State Electricity Regulatory Commission (KERC), prompting opposition leader Pinarayi Vijayan to allege procedural irregularities.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Economy, Power sector, Public Finance

Key Data & Stats: 200 MW, ₹11 per unit, No bids invited, No KERC approval

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Kerala’s electricity demand has been rising, leading the state utility (Kerala State Electricity Board – KSEB) to explore additional power purchases. Under the Electricity Act, 2003, large power procurements require competitive bidding and regulatory clearance from the state electricity regulatory commission to ensure transparency and cost‑effectiveness.
  • Significance: Bypassing the bidding process and regulatory approval can lead to over‑pricing, fiscal stress on the state exchequer, and erosion of public trust in procurement norms. It also raises concerns about adherence to the “single‑buyer” model and the principle of “best value for money” in public procurement.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Potential violation of Section 70 & 71 of the Electricity Act, 2003 which mandate competitive bidding for bulk power purchases.
    • Non‑involvement of KERC may contravene its statutory authority under the Act to fix tariffs and approve power purchase agreements.
    • Risk of fiscal burden if the agreed price exceeds market rates, impacting subsidies and state finances.
    • Legal exposure to public interest litigation (PIL) and audit scrutiny by the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG).
  • Way Forward:
    • Immediate compliance with the Electricity Act: seek retrospective approval from KERC and, if required, re‑open the procurement through a transparent bidding process.
    • Commission an independent audit (CAG or a state-appointed committee) to assess price rationality and procedural lapses.
    • Adopt a standard operating procedure (SOP) for all bulk power purchases, referencing the guidelines of the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) and best practices from the Ministry of Power.
    • Consider formation of a state-level “Power Procurement Review Committee” (akin to an ARC) to oversee large contracts and ensure adherence to competitive procurement norms.

Prelims Pointers

  • Electricity Act, 2003 – Sections 70‑71 (competitive bidding for bulk power purchase).
  • Kerala State Electricity Regulatory Commission (KERC) – statutory authority to approve tariffs and power purchase agreements.
  • Principle of “single‑buyer model” and “best value for money” in public procurement.
  • Potential for Public Interest Litigation (PIL) under Article 32 of the Constitution for violation of statutory procedures.
