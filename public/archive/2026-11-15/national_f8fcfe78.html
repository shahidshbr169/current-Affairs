Democracy based on the Constitution is a model, says Tenginkai

15 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: An MLA inaugurated a taluk‑level Youth Parliament competition in Hubballi on the occasion of the International Day of Democracy. The event aims to inculcate democratic values and parliamentary procedures among young citizens, reinforcing constitutional democracy at the grassroots.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper I – Indian Society & Social Justice (civic participation)

Key Data & Stats: 15 Sep (International Day of Democracy) | Hubballi, Karnataka | Taluk‑level competition

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The United Nations observes 15 September as International Day of Democracy to promote democratic governance worldwide. Indian states and local bodies increasingly organize youth parliament programmes to foster political awareness, following the Ministry of Education’s “Civic Education” guidelines.
  • Significance: Strengthens democratic culture by exposing students to legislative processes, aligns with Article 19(1) (freedom of speech & expression) and Article 21 (right to life with dignity, including participation in public life). Enhances grassroots democratic participation, a key metric in the World Bank’s Governance Indicators.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Limited resources and uneven access across rural‑urban divide may restrict participation. Absence of a standardized curriculum can lead to superficial engagement. Monitoring and evaluation mechanisms are often weak, raising questions about long‑term impact.
  • Way Forward: • Integrate youth parliament modules into the NCERT curriculum under the “Civic Education” framework.
    • Set up a monitoring cell under the State Election Commission to assess outcomes.
    • Leverage recommendations of the ARC on Education (2022) for capacity building of teachers.
    • Encourage public‑private partnerships for funding and mentorship.

Prelims Pointers

  • International Day of Democracy – observed on 15 Sep (UN General Assembly resolution).
  • Constitutional provisions fostering participation: Article 19(1), Article 21, and the concept of “Democratic Decentralisation” under the 73rd & 74th Amendments.
  • Youth Parliament programmes – a flagship initiative of the Ministry of Education’s “Civic Education” scheme.
  • State Election Commission – statutory body that can oversee youth democratic exercises at the taluk level.
