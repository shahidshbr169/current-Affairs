China, Southeast Asia pledge to avoid disputes during talks on South China Sea pact

16 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: China and four ASEAN states – Brunei, Malaysia, the Philippines and Vietnam – reaffirmed their commitment to avoid disputes while negotiating a South China Sea Code of Conduct. The pledge aims to de‑escalate long‑standing territorial standoffs in the strategically vital waterway.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; India’s neighbourhood policy; maritime security and international law.

Key Data & Agreements: South China Sea Code of Conduct (SCOC) talks | Four ASEAN members: Brunei, Malaysia, Philippines, Vietnam | 30% of global maritime trade passes through the SCS

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The SCOC negotiations reflect a shift from unilateral coercion to multilateral confidence‑building, influencing the balance of power in the Indo‑Pacific and testing China’s “peaceful rise” narrative.
  • Impact on India: Stability in the South China Sea safeguards India’s energy imports and trade routes; it also complements India’s “Act East” policy and its own maritime domain awareness initiatives.
  • Way Forward: India should deepen engagement with ASEAN on the SCOC, bolster naval presence in the Indian Ocean Region, and promote a rules‑based order anchored in UNCLOS while offering mediation support.

Prelims Pointers

  • Four ASEAN states involved: Brunei, Malaysia, Philippines, Vietnam.
  • China and Taiwan also claim overlapping zones in the South China Sea.
  • South China Sea Code of Conduct (SCOC) is the primary multilateral framework under ASEAN‑China.
  • The waterway carries 30% of global maritime trade, making it a critical economic artery.
