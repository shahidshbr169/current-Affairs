US sees 34% drop in Indian graduate enrolments but it remains top student source

16 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Graduate enrolments of Indian students in the United States fell by 34%, yet India continues to be the largest source of international students in the US. The rise in Optional Practical Training (OPT) uptake indicates a shift from pure academic pursuits to post‑study employment, prompting policy focus on skill retention and diaspora engagement.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Economic Development (Human Capital, Skill Development, Remittances); GS Paper II – Governance (Education Policy, Ministry of External Affairs, Ministry of Education); GS Paper I – International Relations (India‑US educational ties, diaspora).

Key Data & Stats: 34% drop in Indian graduate enrolments, India remains top student source in US, Rising OPT participation

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Over the past decade, Indian undergraduates and post‑graduates have flocked to US universities due to quality of education, research exposure, and post‑study work options. Recent US immigration reforms, higher tuition, and the pandemic‑induced shift to online learning have curbed new admissions, while the OPT scheme has become a preferred pathway for practical training.
  • Significance: The decline affects India’s human‑capital pipeline, foreign‑exchange earnings from tuition fees, and the strategic India‑US academic partnership. Increased OPT uptake can lead to higher skilled migration, influencing labour market dynamics and remittance inflows.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Potential brain‑drain if OPT converts to long‑term employment abroad.
    • Limited domestic research infrastructure to absorb returning talent.
    • Visa‑policy volatility and rising tuition may deter future aspirants.
    • Inadequate data coordination between Ministry of Education and Ministry of External Affairs on diaspora outcomes.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen the ‘Study in India’ initiative (e.g., scholarships, world‑class research centres).
    • Set up an inter‑ministerial task‑force (Education‑External Affairs‑Commerce) modelled on the ARC to monitor student mobility and skill‑return mechanisms.
    • Negotiate bilateral agreements for smoother OPT‑to‑employment transition with return‑of‑service clauses.
    • Enhance data‑analytics capability to track alumni impact and design targeted skill‑upgradation programmes.

Prelims Pointers

  • Indian students are the largest international student cohort in the United States.
  • Graduate enrolments fell by 34% in the latest reporting period.
  • Optional Practical Training (OPT) is witnessing higher participation among Indian graduates.
  • Policy relevance: Ministry of Education’s ‘Study in India’ scheme; Ministry of External Affairs’ diaspora engagement.
