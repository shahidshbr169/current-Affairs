Police should not stereotype DJ parties, says Kerala High Court

16 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Kerala High Court cautioned that police are crossing the “fine line between policing and police raj” by automatically equating DJ parties with drug‑related offences. It directed that law‑enforcement must avoid stereotyping such gatherings and must base action on concrete evidence.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Law and Justice (police powers, fundamental rights).

Key Data & Stats: Kerala High Court judgment (2026)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Recent police raids on DJ events across Kerala resulted in drug seizures and arrests, prompting criticism that the police were treating all such events as drug hotspots without specific intelligence.
  • Significance: The observation underscores the tension between maintaining public order and safeguarding constitutional freedoms such as the right to peaceful assembly and expression. It also highlights the broader debate on “police raj” versus accountable policing.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Lack of clear SOPs for policing cultural events leads to discretionary action.
    • Potential violation of Article 21 (right to life & liberty) and Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of speech & expression).
    • Stigmatization may push legitimate entertainment venues underground, complicating genuine drug‑control efforts.
  • Way Forward: • Formulate state‑level guidelines on policing of public entertainment, drawing on recommendations of the Supreme Court in D.K. Basu vs. State of West Bengal (1997) for procedural safeguards.
    • Strengthen the Police Complaints Authority (PCA) in Kerala for independent redressal of misuse of power.
    • Conduct regular sensitisation and human‑rights training for police officers.
    • Involve community‑policing models and local cultural bodies in drafting event‑specific security protocols.

Prelims Pointers

  • Article 21 & Article 19(1)(a) – protection against arbitrary police action.
  • Police Reforms Committee (2005) – recommends separation of investigation & law‑and‑order functions.
  • Kerala Police Act – governs powers and duties of the state police.
  • Supreme Court judgment D.K. Basu vs. State of West Bengal (1997) – procedural safeguards against police excesses.
