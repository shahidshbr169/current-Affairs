Punjab anti-drug activist Jhota's autopsy conducted at PGIMER, cremation on Nov. 8

7 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Anti‑drug activist Parvinder Singh Jhota’s body was examined at the Post‑Graduate Institute of Medical Education & Research (PGIMER) and his family has scheduled the cremation for Nov 8 2026 at around 12 noon, as announced by religious leader Nihang Raja Raj Singh. The episode underscores the need for transparent forensic procedures and robust drug‑control governance in Punjab.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Internal Security, Law & Order, Drug Abuse & Narcotics.

Key Data & Stats: Nov 8 2026 – Cremation date | PGIMER – Designated autopsy centre in Chandigarh

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Punjab has been grappling with a chronic drug‑abuse crisis, prompting civil‑society activists like Jhota to campaign for stricter enforcement of the NDPS Act. His sudden death has triggered calls for an impartial autopsy to rule out foul play.
  • Significance: The handling of the autopsy and cremation reflects on state accountability, the credibility of forensic institutions, and the broader governance challenge of curbing narcotics while safeguarding activists’ rights.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Potential political interference, limited forensic capacity, and the absence of an independent oversight mechanism can compromise the integrity of post‑mortem reports. Moreover, the lack of a clear protocol for high‑profile cases may erode public trust.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen forensic infrastructure by upgrading state‑run mortuary labs and ensuring ISO‑certified standards.
    • Constitute an independent “Forensic Oversight Committee” (modeled on the Sarkaria Commission’s recommendation for neutral bodies) to review autopsies in sensitive cases.
    • Fast‑track courts under the NDPS Act to expedite investigations related to drug‑related deaths.
    • Expand community‑based rehabilitation programmes and integrate them with the National Action Plan on Drug Demand Reduction.

Prelims Pointers

  • NDPS Act, 1985 – statutory framework for drug control.
  • Article 21 of the Constitution – Right to life & personal liberty, relevant to activist safety.
  • PGIMER – Apex medical institute authorized for forensic autopsies in Chandigarh.
  • Punjab Drug Abuse Monitoring System – State‑level mechanism tracking narcotics trends.
