A sub-adult elephant found dead in Shankara Range in Shivamogga

7 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: A sub‑adult elephant was found dead in the Shankara Range of Shivamogga, Karnataka. Officials suspect the death resulted from electrocution after the animal touched a live power line bordering a paddy field, highlighting the growing human‑wildlife conflict in forest‑agricultural interfaces.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment & Ecology; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Wildlife Protection Act, 1972; Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change)

Key Data & Stats: Shankara Range, Shivamogda, Karnataka | Sub‑adult elephant (≈ 2–3 years old) | Electrocution suspected

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Rapid expansion of agriculture and power infrastructure in the Western Ghats has increased the overlap between elephant habitats and human settlements. Karnataka records one of the highest incidences of elephant‑human conflict in India, with frequent incidents of crop raiding, road‑kill, and electrocution.
  • Significance: The incident underscores gaps in wildlife‑friendly infrastructure planning and the effectiveness of existing legal safeguards (e.g., Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, Schedule I species). It also raises concerns about biodiversity loss, community safety, and the implementation of the National Elephant Action Plan (NEAP).
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Inadequate routing of high‑voltage lines through elephant corridors.
    • Absence of insulated or underground cabling in high‑risk zones.
    • Limited coordination between the State Electricity Board, Forest Department, and local panchayats.
    • Deficient compensation and rapid response mechanisms for wildlife fatalities.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a joint expert committee (including representatives from the Ministry of Power, MoEFCC, Karnataka Forest Department, and NBWL) to audit and redesign power line routes in identified elephant corridors.
    • Adopt insulated conductors or underground cabling in high‑conflict zones as per the guidelines of the Central Electricity Authority.
    • Strengthen implementation of the NEAP by establishing real‑time monitoring (e.g., GIS‑based corridor mapping) and rapid response teams.
    • Promote community‑based mitigation measures such as solar‑powered fencing and early‑warning systems.
    • Ensure strict enforcement of the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, with penalties for negligence by utility agencies.

Prelims Pointers

  • Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 – Schedule I species include the Indian elephant.
  • National Elephant Action Plan (NEAP) – aims to reduce human‑elephant conflict and mortality.
  • Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) – regulatory authority for wildlife conservation.
  • Constitutional provision – Article 48A (State’s duty to protect the environment) and Article 21 (right to life, extended by courts to include wildlife protection).
