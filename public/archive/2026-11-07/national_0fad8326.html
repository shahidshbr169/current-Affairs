Enforcement Directorate is not a ‘super cop’, says Karnataka High Court

7 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Karnataka High Court held that the Enforcement Directorate (ED) is not a “super‑cop” and cannot lodge a private complaint when other investigating agencies refuse to act on its information. Consequently, the court quashed the ED’s criminal cases against Congress MLA K.Y. Nanjegowda of Malur.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Law and Justice (investigative agencies, judicial review).

Key Data & Stats: Karnataka High Court judgment ED cases against K.Y. Nanjegowda quashed

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The ED, empowered under the Prevention of Money‑Laundering Act, 2002, had initiated investigations against the MLA based on information it received. When other agencies (e.g., police, CBI) declined to register a case, the ED attempted to file a private complaint, a move challenged before the High Court.
  • Significance: The judgment delineates the limits of the ED’s investigative powers, reinforcing the principle that no single agency can bypass procedural safeguards or act as a “super‑cop.” It safeguards federalism and the rule of law by ensuring that agency actions remain subject to judicial scrutiny.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Ambiguities in the PMLA regarding the ED’s authority to file private complaints create procedural bottlenecks. Over‑reach can lead to misuse of the agency’s powers, eroding public confidence and potentially politicising investigations.
  • Way Forward: • Clarify statutory provisions through an amendment to the PMLA, explicitly restricting the ED’s power to file private complaints.
    • Constitute a review committee (e.g., under the Attorney‑General’s Office) to examine inter‑agency coordination mechanisms.
    • Adopt guidelines similar to the Sarkaria Commission’s recommendations on centre‑state balance, ensuring that state‑level investigations are not unduly overridden by central agencies.

Prelims Pointers

  • Enforcement Directorate – investigative wing under the Ministry of Finance, functioning under the Prevention of Money‑Laundering Act, 2002.
  • High Court’s power of judicial review over administrative actions of central agencies.
  • Concept of “private complaint” – not permissible for the ED under existing statutory framework.
  • Implications for federal structure – checks on central agencies operating in states.
