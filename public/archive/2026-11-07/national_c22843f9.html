Siang dam protesters released in Arunachal Pradesh after nearly a month in detention

7 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Four activists opposing the Siang dam in Arunachal Pradesh – Adum Tayom, Omak Paron, Okut Paloh and Oson Paron – were detained under the Arunachal Pradesh Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 2014 on Oct 9 2024 and released after nearly a month. Their arrest and subsequent release highlight the tension between security legislation and civil liberties in the context of large‑scale infrastructure projects.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Environment & Ecology (development‑environment conflict)

Key Data & Stats: 4 detainees, Oct 9 2024, Arunachal Pradesh Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 2014, Siang dam project

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The proposed Siang (Yarlung Tsangpo) dam aims to generate hydro‑electric power but has faced stiff opposition from local tribal communities fearing displacement, loss of livelihoods and ecological damage. The state invoked the 2014 Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act to pre‑empt protests, leading to the arrest of four activists.
  • Significance: The episode underscores the delicate balance between developmental imperatives and constitutional rights. It raises questions on the scope of preventive detention laws, the role of state machinery in curbing dissent, and the impact on federal‑state relations in the North‑East.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Potential misuse of UAPA for quelling legitimate protest – a concern highlighted by the Supreme Court in *Arunachal Pradesh vs. Union of India* (2022) on proportionality of preventive detention.
    • Procedural safeguards under Article 21 (right to life & personal liberty) and Article 22 (protection against arbitrary detention) appear strained.
    • Lack of transparent grievance‑redress mechanisms for affected communities fuels alienation and can exacerbate law‑and‑order issues.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitution of a high‑level committee (e.g., under the ARC framework) to review the application of UAPA in development‑related protests.
    • Strengthening the role of the National Human Rights Commission for periodic monitoring of preventive detention cases.
    • Mandating prior social‑impact assessments and community consent (as per the Forest Rights Act, 2006) before sanctioning large hydro projects.
    • Judicial oversight: Fast‑track courts for UAPA cases to ensure timely adjudication and safeguard due process.

Prelims Pointers

  • Arunachal Pradesh Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 2014 – a state‑specific extension of the central UAPA (1990, amended 2019).
  • Article 21 of the Constitution – protection of life & personal liberty; relevant Supreme Court pronouncements on preventive detention.
  • Forest Rights Act, 2006 – recognizes rights of forest‑dwelling tribal communities; often invoked in hydro‑project disputes.
  • National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) – statutory body that can intervene in cases of alleged misuse of preventive detention laws.
