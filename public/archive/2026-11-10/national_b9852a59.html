Supreme Court goes beyond Deepavali AQI, flags vehicular emissions polluting Delhi air

10 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Supreme Court has taken cognizance of the deteriorating air quality in Delhi during Deepavali, highlighting that vehicular emissions from Delhi and adjoining NCR states are a major contributor. It has asked the authorities to detail measures taken to regulate entry and movement of out‑of‑state vehicles, signalling judicial intervention in urban pollution governance.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment, Ecology & Biodiversity; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Judicial Review, Article 21, statutory bodies)

Key Data & Stats: Deepavali AQI spike Delhi NCR vehicular share ≈ 40 % of total emissions (est.)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Delhi’s air quality consistently breaches the National Ambient Air Quality Standards, with episodic spikes during festivals. The National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) 2019‑23 targets a 20‑30 % reduction in PM₂.₅, yet vehicular emissions remain a stubborn source, compounded by inter‑state traffic.
  • Significance: Air pollution infringes on the right to life under Article 21 of the Constitution and triggers public‑health costs. Judicial scrutiny underscores the need for coordinated federal‑state action, especially where jurisdictional overlap hampers regulation.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Fragmented authority: Delhi’s traffic police vs. state transport departments of Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan.
    • Implementation gaps in NCAP’s “Vehicle Emission Control” sub‑mission, including delayed retro‑fitting of diesel engines and weak enforcement of entry‑restriction norms.
    • Legal ambiguity on the extent of Supreme Court’s power to direct inter‑state vehicular movement without a legislative mandate.
  • Way Forward:
    • Form a joint “Delhi‑NCR Air‑Quality Task Force” under the Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change with representation from all bordering states.
    • Adopt a “green corridor” policy: restrict entry of high‑emission vehicles during peak pollution periods; promote electric buses and CNG‑based intra‑city transport.
    • Empower the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) to issue binding “No‑Entry” directives under the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981.
    • Periodic review by an independent expert committee (e.g., chaired by a former Supreme Court judge) to assess compliance and recommend corrective measures.

Prelims Pointers

  • Supreme Court’s intervention under the doctrine of “public interest litigation” (PIL) for environmental protection.
  • Article 21 – Right to life includes the right to a healthy environment.
  • Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981; Environment Protection Act, 1986.
  • National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) – target 20‑30 % reduction in PM₂.₅ by 2024‑25.
