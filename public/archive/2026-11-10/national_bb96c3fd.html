AIKS national conference to be held in Nalgonda from November 15 to 18

10 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The All India Kisan Sabha (AIKS) will convene a national conference in Nalgonda from 15‑18 Nov 2026. Around 1 lakh farmers are expected to attend a public meeting on 15 Nov 2026 to discuss farm suicides, demand for remunerative prices and the impact of international trade agreements on Indian agriculture.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Agriculture, Food Processing, Rural Development; GS Paper IV – Ethics (farmers’ welfare, policy‑making).

Key Data & Stats: 1 lakh farmers expected; 15‑18 Nov 2026 conference dates; Nalgonda, Telangana venue.

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: AIKS, the farmer wing of the CPI(M), has been vocal on agrarian distress, especially after a series of high‑profile farm suicides and concerns that WTO‑linked trade deals (e.g., RCEP, US‑India Trade Agreement) could erode price support mechanisms.
  • Significance: The gathering underscores the growing demand for policy reforms such as higher Minimum Support Prices (MSP), better credit flow, and safeguards against adverse effects of global trade on small and marginal farmers – issues central to food security and rural employment.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Fragmented farmer representation makes consensus on price policy difficult.
    • Existing legal framework (e.g., Agricultural Produce Market Committee Acts) may limit price realization.
    • International trade commitments could constrain the government’s ability to raise MSP without breaching WTO obligations.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a high‑level committee (similar to the Commission on Agricultural Prices) with representation from AIKS, Ministry of Agriculture, RBI and trade experts to review MSP and export‑import policies.
    • Utilise the Committee on the Impact of WTO Agreements on Agriculture (if formed) to assess trade‑off between market access and farmer welfare.
    • Strengthen the National Commission for Farmers (under the Ministry of Agriculture) to monitor farm‑suicide trends and recommend psychosocial interventions.
    • Promote state‑level “Remunerative Price” schemes under Article 243 (1) of the Constitution (Panchayati Raj) to ensure price parity for perishable crops.

Prelims Pointers

  • All India Kisan Sabha (AIKS) – farmer’s organization linked to left parties.
  • Farm suicides – a key indicator of agrarian distress; often cited in reports of the National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB).
  • Remunerative Price / Minimum Support Price (MSP) – central government price support mechanism under the Food Corporation of India (FCI) Act.
  • International trade agreements – RCEP, US‑India Trade Agreement; their implications for Indian agriculture under WTO commitments.
