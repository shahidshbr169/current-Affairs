Domestic rooftop solar installations in T.N. remain low due to policy challenges, say consumers

10 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Consumer groups in Tamil Nadu report that domestic rooftop solar installations remain markedly low, attributing the stagnation to ambiguous state policies, delayed approvals and unfavourable net‑metering tariffs. The slowdown hampers India’s renewable‑energy targets and curtails household energy‑cost savings.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment & Ecology (Renewable Energy, Climate Change); GS Paper II – Governance & Polity (State‑level policy formulation, regulatory bodies)

Key Data & Stats: No specific quantitative data provided

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The National Solar Mission envisages 20 GW of rooftop solar capacity by 2022‑23. Tamil Nadu, a leading solar‑resource state, introduced a state‑level net‑metering policy in 2020, but frequent revisions and unclear subsidy mechanisms have deterred homeowners.
  • Significance: Accelerated rooftop solar can reduce peak‑load pressure on the grid, lower household electricity bills, and contribute to India’s commitment under the Paris Agreement (NDCs). It also creates local jobs in installation and maintenance.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Policy volatility – frequent changes in tariff caps and eligibility criteria.
    • Cumbersome clearance process involving multiple agencies (TNERC, local bodies, distribution utilities).
    • Lack of transparent subsidy disbursement and delayed net‑metering approvals.
    • Absence of a dedicated state‑level rooftop solar fund.
  • Way Forward: • Enact a stable, time‑bound net‑metering framework through the Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission (TNERC).
    • Set up a single‑window clearance portal for permits and approvals.
    • Introduce a state‑backed rooftop solar incentive fund, modelled on the Central Financial Assistance (CFA) scheme.
    • Periodic review by a committee comprising representatives from MNRE, TNERC, consumer groups and industry (akin to an ARC‑type advisory panel) to ensure policy continuity.

Prelims Pointers

  • National Solar Mission – target of 20 GW rooftop solar by 2022‑23.
  • Key regulatory body: Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission (TNERC).
  • Policy instrument: Net‑metering guidelines (State‑level amendments since 2020).
  • Relevant constitutional provision: Article 48A – State’s duty to protect the environment.
