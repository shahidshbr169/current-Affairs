Illegal bus network in Delhi? Operators claim payments, advance alerts before checks

10 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: An undercover Aaj Tak report captured private bus operators in Delhi claiming they could continue running “restricted” buses by paying bribes and receiving advance alerts before police checks. The revelation highlights systemic lapses in transport regulation, enforcement and potential corruption within the capital’s public‑transport ecosystem.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Regulatory bodies, public‑sector undertakings, corruption & accountability); GS Paper III – Technology & Infrastructure (Urban transport management).

Key Data & Stats: No specific quantitative data disclosed in the report

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Delhi’s bus network comprises Delhi Transport Corporation (DTC) services and a large fleet of private operators licensed under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. Over the years, the government has imposed “restricted” routes and timings to de‑congest the city, but enforcement has been uneven, creating opportunities for illicit operations.
  • Significance: Illegal bus services undermine road safety, erode public revenue, and compromise the credibility of transport regulatory mechanisms. They also reflect broader governance challenges such as rent‑seeking, weak monitoring, and collusion between operators and enforcement agencies.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Inadequate real‑time monitoring of fleet movements; reliance on manual checks.
    • Opaque licensing and renewal processes that can be manipulated.
    • Potential conflict of interest within local transport authorities and police, leading to selective enforcement.
    • Lack of a robust whistle‑blower protection framework for transport‑sector employees.
  • Way Forward:
    • Introduce GPS‑based tracking for all commercial buses and integrate data with the Transport Department’s command‑center.
    • Set up an independent oversight committee (e.g., under the aegis of the Comptroller and Auditor General) to audit private bus licences and revenue streams.
    • Empower the Central Vigilance Commission or CBI to investigate allegations of collusion between operators and enforcement officials.
    • Adopt a “single‑window” digital platform for licence renewal, with transparent criteria and automated background checks.
    • Periodic review by an ARC‑type committee on urban transport to recommend structural reforms and capacity‑building for the Delhi Transport Corporation.

Prelims Pointers

  • Delhi Transport Corporation (DTC) – statutory body under the Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs.
  • Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 – governs licensing, registration and operation of commercial vehicles.
  • Article 243P of the Constitution – empowers local bodies (e.g., Delhi Municipal Corporation) in matters of urban transport.
  • Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) – apex anti‑corruption authority that can be approached for systemic irregularities.
