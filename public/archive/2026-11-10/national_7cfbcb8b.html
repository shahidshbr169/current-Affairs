Policy to promote growth of civil aviation in Karnataka to be placed before next meeting of State Cabinet for clearance

10 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Karnataka’s Industries and Infrastructure Development Ministry will table a policy aimed at accelerating civil aviation growth in the state at the upcoming Cabinet meeting. The move follows the suspension of flight operations at Shivamogga Airport, blamed on gaps in the Union Government’s UDAN‑2 regional connectivity framework.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Centre‑State relations, statutory bodies); GS Paper III – Economic Development (Civil aviation, infrastructure, regional connectivity)

Key Data & Stats: Shivamogga Airport – services suspended UDAN‑2 – second phase of Regional Connectivity Scheme

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Regional Connectivity Scheme (RCS) – popularly known as UDAN – was launched in 2016 to make air travel affordable and connect unserved/underserved airports. UDAN‑2, the second phase, seeks to expand the network but has faced implementation bottlenecks, especially in coordination between the Ministry of Civil Aviation and state governments.
  • Significance: Enhanced civil aviation can spur tourism, trade, and investment in tier‑2/3 cities like Shivamogga, reduce regional disparities, and generate employment. For Karnataka, a robust aviation policy aligns with its broader industrial growth agenda and the “Make in India” thrust.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Policy‑implementation gap: UDUD‑2 guidelines lack clear fiscal de‑linkage for state‑run airports, leading to funding shortfalls.
    • Centre‑State coordination: Article 246 of the Constitution allocates aviation to the Union List, limiting state autonomy in airport operations.
    • Infrastructure deficits: Runway upgrades, ATC facilities, and ground handling at smaller airports remain inadequate.
    • Commercial viability: Low passenger load factors make airlines reluctant to operate without viable subsidies.
  • Way Forward:
    • Set up a joint Centre‑State Aviation Coordination Committee (akin to the ARC) to resolve policy‑implementation issues.
    • Introduce a performance‑linked subsidy model under UDAN‑2, with clear benchmarks for passenger traffic.
    • Leverage the National Civil Aviation Policy (2023) to streamline land‑lease, tax incentives, and PPP models for regional airports.
    • Prioritise capacity building of state aviation authorities and adopt a phased runway‑modernisation plan.

Prelims Pointers

  • UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Naagrik) – Regional Connectivity Scheme, Phase‑2 (UDAN‑2).
  • Article 246 of the Constitution – Union List includes Civil Aviation.
  • Ministry of Civil Aviation – nodal agency for UDAN implementation.
  • State’s role under the National Civil Aviation Policy, 2023 – PPPs, land‑lease, fiscal incentives.
