Cyclones Arnab, Murasu damage 13.21% of Krishna district’s paddy area

10 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Cyclones Arnab and Murasu battered Krishna district of Andhra Pradesh, destroying 13.21% of the district’s paddy area. Farmers who incur a minimum 33% crop loss will be paid a compensation of ₹25,000 per farmer, with the worst damage reported in the coastal mandals.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Agriculture, Food Security & Disaster Management; GS Paper II – Governance, Policy Implementation & Public Administration.

Key Data & Stats: 13.21% paddy area damaged, ₹25,000 compensation per eligible farmer, 33% crop loss threshold, Cyclones Arnab & Murasu (2024)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Bay of Bengal cyclone season has intensified due to climate change, leading to frequent land‑falling cyclones. Andhra Pradesh’s agrarian economy, heavily reliant on paddy cultivation, is vulnerable to such extreme weather events. Existing safety nets include the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) and state‑level disaster relief funds.
  • Significance: Crop loss directly threatens food security, farmer incomes, and rural employment. The damage also strains state exchequer resources and tests the efficacy of disaster‑relief mechanisms, making it a litmus test for governance and resilience policies.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Verification of the 33% loss threshold can be time‑consuming, leading to delays in payout.
    • The fixed compensation of ₹25,000 may be inadequate against market price fluctuations and input costs.
    • Overlap and coordination gaps between central schemes (PMFBY) and state relief measures can cause duplication or omission.
    • Limited adoption of climate‑smart agriculture and inadequate early‑warning infrastructure in coastal mandals.
  • Way Forward:
    • Strengthen PMFBY coverage by simplifying claim procedures and linking it with the state’s compensation scheme.
    • Establish a fast‑track verification cell under the State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA) to expedite the 33% loss assessment.
    • Promote climate‑resilient varieties, System of Rice Intensification (SRI), and alternate cropping in vulnerable mandals.
    • Adopt ARC‑recommended integrated disaster risk reduction: improve coastal embankments, expand early‑warning systems, and create a dedicated cyclone‑relief contingency fund.
    • Encourage public‑private partnerships for post‑disaster agri‑logistics and market linkage to minimise post‑harvest losses.

Prelims Pointers

  • Cyclones Arnab & Murasu – 2024 Bay of Bengal cyclones affecting Andhra Pradesh.
  • Krishna district – coastal mandals most vulnerable; 13.21% paddy area damaged.
  • Compensation framework – ₹25,000 per farmer for ≥33% crop loss; linked to state disaster relief.
  • Relevant bodies – Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers’ Welfare, State Disaster Management Authority, Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY).
