D.K. Shivakumar places Karnataka’s demands on stage with with PM

13 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Karnataka Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar used the meeting with the Prime Minister to press four key demands – a more equitable financial share for Bengaluru’s Namma Metro, swift clearance of the Mekedatu water‑ diversion project, repeal of the Kasturirangan Report on river‑water allocation, and immediate drought‑relief measures for the state.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Centre‑State Relations, Finance Commission, Urban Development; GS Paper III – Water Resources, Inter‑State River Disputes, Disaster Management.

Key Data & Stats: 2024

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Karnataka’s Namma Metro has been funded through a cost‑sharing model where the state bears a larger share than other metros, prompting calls for a revised financial partnership. The Mekedatu project, a proposed Cauvery‑river diversion, has been stalled due to inter‑state clearance issues. The Kasturirangan Report (2007) recommended a specific water‑sharing formula that Karnataka now seeks to discard, citing changed hydrological realities. Prolonged droughts in 2023‑24 have intensified demand for central relief.
  • Significance: The demands touch upon fiscal federalism (fair devolution of funds for urban infrastructure), water security (critical for agriculture and industry), and disaster response (drought relief). Their resolution will affect the Centre’s ability to balance national priorities with state aspirations, a core theme of cooperative federalism.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Financial partnership: Limited central budget and competing metro projects may restrict additional grants. • Mekedatu: Ongoing legal disputes with Tamil Nadu and environmental clearances pose procedural bottlenecks. • Kasturirangan Report: Repeal would require renegotiation of the Cauvery Water Dispute Tribunal awards, potentially inviting litigation. • Drought relief: Allocation of central schemes (e.g., PM‑Kisan, Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana) may be delayed by bureaucratic procedures.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute an inter‑state water‑resource committee under the ARC framework to reassess Cauvery allocations. • The 15th Finance Commission could be urged to recommend a revised metro‑funding formula for high‑density cities. • Fast‑track environmental clearances for Mekedatu through a joint Centre‑State task force. • Deploy targeted drought‑relief measures via existing schemes (e.g., PM‑Garib Kalyan Yojana) and consider a special central assistance package for Karnataka’s arid districts.

Prelims Pointers

  • Article 275 of the Constitution – Union’s duty to provide financial assistance to states.
  • Finance Commission (15th) – role in recommending devolution for urban infrastructure.
  • Kasturirangan Report (2007) – water‑sharing recommendations for Cauvery basin.
  • Mekedatu project – inter‑state water‑diversion scheme between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.
