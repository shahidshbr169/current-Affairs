Shashi Tharoor alleges 115 Bharat Summit speakers barred entry to India, their visas cancelled

13 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Senior Congress leader Shashi Tharoor claimed that visas of 115 Bharat Summit speakers were cancelled, preventing them from entering India. The summit aims to launch the “Our World Institute” – a think‑tank intended to foster dialogue between the Global South and the Global North, making the visa issue a matter of diplomatic and policy relevance.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; International Relations – India’s foreign policy, soft power, and diplomatic protocols.

Key Data & Stats: 115 speakers barred | Bharat Summit – proposed think‑tank “Our World Institute”

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Bharat Summit, announced by the Ministry of External Affairs, is positioned as a platform for Global South‑North engagement. Visa issuance in India is governed by the Passports Act 1967 and the Foreigners (Regulation of Entry) Order 2020, administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) and the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA).
  • Significance: Cancellation of visas for a large cohort of international experts can dent India’s soft‑power credentials, affect the credibility of the proposed think‑tank, and raise questions about procedural transparency in the exercise of sovereign entry powers.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Legal: Potential conflict with constitutional guarantees under Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of speech & expression) and Article 21 (right to life & personal liberty) when the restriction is not demonstrably in the public interest.
    • Procedural: Lack of a clear, publicly disclosed criteria for visa cancellation may violate principles of natural justice and administrative fairness.
    • Diplomatic: May strain India’s relations with countries represented by the barred speakers and affect future participation in similar global forums.
  • Way Forward:
    • Form a high‑level inter‑ministerial committee (MEA‑MHA) to review visa‑cancellation protocols for international conferences.
    • Adopt transparent guidelines, possibly through a Parliamentary Committee on Foreign Affairs, ensuring compliance with constitutional safeguards.
    • Consider an independent review by the Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) to recommend a balanced visa‑policy framework that safeguards security while promoting India’s soft‑power agenda.

Prelims Pointers

  • Visa issuance governed by the Passports Act 1967 and the Foreigners (Regulation of Entry) Order 2020.
  • Constitutional provisions relevant – Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of speech) and Article 21 (personal liberty).
  • Think‑tank “Our World Institute” – envisaged as a platform for Global South‑North dialogue.
  • Potential role of the Parliamentary Committee on Foreign Affairs and ARC in framing transparent visa policies.
