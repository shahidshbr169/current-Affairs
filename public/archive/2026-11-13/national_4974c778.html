Trees misidentified, misjudged, wrongly marked and felled to facilitate civil projects in Hyderabad

13 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Approximately 1800+ trees are being cut down for an access road to Cherlapally Railway Station in Hyderabad. Field verification shows that most felled trees do not match the species listed in the approved clearance, indicating serious procedural lapses in environmental clearances.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment, Ecology and Biodiversity; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Forest Conservation Act, 1980; Article 48A)

Key Data & Stats: 1800+ trees felled, Cherlapally Railway Station access road, Species mismatch with permission copy

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Under the Forest Conservation Act, 1980, any felling of trees in forest or non‑forest land exceeding a prescribed limit requires prior clearance from the Ministry of Environment, Forests & Climate Change (MoEFCC). Urban infrastructure projects often seek “tree‑felling permission” from the State Forest Department, which is subject to verification by the State Pollution Control Board and, if contested, the National Green Tribunal (NGT).
  • Significance: Unauthorised removal of a large number of trees undermines urban green cover, aggravates air‑quality issues, and contravenes the Directive Principle of State Policy (Article 48A) to protect and improve the environment. It also sets a precedent for regulatory evasion in fast‑growing cities, affecting climate‑change mitigation targets.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Inaccurate or falsified species data in the clearance document points to weak verification mechanisms.
    • Coordination gaps between the municipal corporation, HMDA, and the Forest Department enable “green‑washing” of project proposals.
    • Limited real‑time monitoring; reliance on manual field inspections delays detection of violations.
    • Legal ambiguity regarding “compensatory afforestation” when the species planted differ from those removed.
  • Way Forward:
    • Adopt satellite‑based remote sensing and GIS dashboards for continuous monitoring of tree‑felling permits (as recommended by the ARC on Urban Forestry).
    • Mandate third‑party audits of clearance documents before project commencement; non‑compliance to attract penal provisions under the Forest Conservation Act.
    • Strengthen the role of the NGT by fast‑tracking petitions related to urban tree‑felling and imposing stricter compensation ratios.
    • Form a joint oversight committee comprising HMDA, State Forest Department, and local NGOs to review all infrastructure‑related clearances.

Prelims Pointers

  • Forest Conservation Act, 1980 – requires prior clearance for felling > 0.5 ha of forest land.
  • Article 48A of the Constitution – State shall protect and improve the environment.
  • National Green Tribunal (NGT) – adjudicates environmental disputes and can stay illegal felling.
  • Hyderabad Metropolitan Development Authority (HMDA) – agency responsible for urban infrastructure projects.
