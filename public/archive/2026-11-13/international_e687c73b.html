China stages unannounced Pacific drills in ‘silent intimidation’ of Taiwan, officials say

13 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: China conducted unannounced naval and coast‑guard drills in the Pacific, deploying over 100 vessels in a show of force aimed at intimidating Taiwan, according to senior Taiwanese security officials.

Key Data & Agreements: >100 vessels mobilised; drills held early this month; no formal treaty invoked, but reflects China’s “gray zone” tactics under its “Anti‑Access/Area Denial” (A2/AD) doctrine.

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The exercise underscores China’s willingness to employ coercive maritime maneuvers without formal declarations of war, heightening tensions in the Indo‑Pacific and challenging the rules‑based order championed by the US, Japan, Australia and India (Quad).
  • Impact on India: Escalation near Taiwan could disrupt sea‑lane security for Indian trade, affect the strategic calculus of the Quad, and compel India to recalibrate its naval deployments and diplomatic outreach in the region.
  • Way Forward: India should deepen maritime cooperation with Quad partners, enhance its own A2/AD capabilities, pursue confidence‑building measures with China, and advocate for a multilateral maritime code of conduct in the South China Sea and adjacent waters.
