Justice Karnan’s incident brought judicial misconduct to the forefront

13 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The controversy surrounding Justice Karnan has spotlighted alleged judicial misconduct, prompting calls to overhaul the judge‑selection mechanism for constitutional courts and to devise an alternative, less‑political process for removing errant judges.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; Judicial Reforms; Constitutional Bodies

Key Data & Stats: N/A

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts are appointed under Articles 124, 217 and the 1993 Supreme Court Judges (Appointment) Act, currently through the collegium system. The National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) Act 2015 was struck down by the Supreme Court, reinstating the collegium. Removal of a judge remains limited to impeachment under Article 124(4), a process rarely invoked.
  • Significance: Public confidence in the judiciary hinges on both competence and accountability. Perceived impunity for misconduct can erode the rule of law and affect the balance of power among the three branches of government.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: The collegium’s opacity fuels allegations of nepotism; impeachment is cumbersome, requiring a two‑thirds majority in both Houses and a judicial inquiry, making it ineffective for timely redress. Absence of a statutory, transparent mechanism for dealing with misconduct creates a governance gap.
  • Way Forward: • Set up a Judicial Appointments and Accountability Commission (JAAC) with representation from the judiciary, executive, legislature and civil society (similar to recommendations of the 2014 Justice B.N. Kirpal Committee).
    • Institutionalise a transparent, merit‑based selection protocol with publicly disclosed criteria and a fixed tenure for collegium members.
    • Enact a statutory removal procedure—e.g., a Judicial Conduct Tribunal with powers to recommend suspension or removal, subject to parliamentary oversight, thereby reducing reliance on impeachment.
    • Periodic review by a constitutional amendment committee (ARC‑type) to assess the efficacy of the new mechanism.

Prelims Pointers

  • Article 124(4) – Impeachment of Supreme Court judges.
  • Article 217 – Appointment of High Court judges.
  • Collegium system – Current de‑facto method for judicial appointments.
  • National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) Act 2015 – Repealed by Supreme Court (Supreme Court Judges (Appointment) and Transfer) (Amendment) Act 2015.
  • Judicial Standards and Accountability Bill – Proposed legislation for a statutory removal process.
