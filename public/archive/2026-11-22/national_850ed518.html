'Let's Discuss Easier Issues First': Pakistan Minister Musadik Malik On Indus Waters Treaty

22 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: On 23 April 2025, India announced that it is placing the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty (IWT) in abeyance following the Pahalgam terror attack in Jammu & Kashmir. The move signals a major shift in the bilateral water‑sharing regime with Pakistan and raises concerns over regional water security.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – International Relations, Water Resources Management, Treaties & Agreements; GS Paper III – Security & Strategic Issues; GS Paper I – Geography (River basins)

Key Data & Stats: Indus Waters Treaty – 1960, Abeyance declared – 23 Apr 2025, Pahalgam terror attack – 2025

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The IWT, brokered by the World Bank after the 1960 Indo‑Pak war, allocated the three western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan and the three eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India, with a detailed dispute‑resolution mechanism (Indus Commission, neutral experts, arbitration).
  • Significance: Water from the Indus basin sustains agriculture, hydro‑electricity and domestic needs for over 300 million people across both nations. An abeyance threatens irrigation schedules, power generation, and could exacerbate bilateral tensions, impacting regional stability and India’s diplomatic standing.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Legal ambiguity: The treaty does not expressly provide for “abeyance,” raising questions under international law and the treaty’s dispute‑resolution clauses.
    • Security‑policy overlap: Linking a terror incident to a water treaty may set a precedent for politicising technical agreements.
    • Economic impact: Potential disruption of water releases could affect crop yields in Punjab, Haryana and Pakistan’s Punjab, with downstream socio‑economic repercussions.
  • Way Forward:
    • Activate the IWT’s existing dispute‑resolution mechanism – convene the Indus Commission and seek neutral expert opinion to de‑escalate.
    • Consider a joint technical committee, possibly chaired by a neutral third‑party (World Bank/UN) to monitor water releases during the security fallout.
    • Long‑term: Initiate a review of the treaty under a high‑level bilateral water‑security dialogue, involving the Ministry of External Affairs, Ministry of Water Resources, and think‑tanks like the ARC for integrated river‑basin management.

Prelims Pointers

  • Indus Waters Treaty (1960) – bilateral water‑sharing agreement brokered by the World Bank.
  • Key provisions: allocation of western vs. eastern rivers, Indus Commission, neutral‑expert and arbitration mechanisms.
  • Abeyance declared on 23 Apr 2025 after the Pahalgam terror attack.
  • Strategic relevance: water security, Indo‑Pak bilateral relations, and implications for regional stability.
