China ran 2-year spy campaign to acquire advanced AI chips, evade US curbs: Report

22 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: China’s intelligence agency conducted a covert two‑year operation to procure cutting‑edge AI chips and chart global semiconductor supply chains. The campaign underscores Beijing’s drive for technological self‑reliance as Western nations tighten export curbs on advanced semiconductors.

In response, Beijing’s Ministry of State Security launched a systematic espionage drive to acquire the technology and map the worldwide supply chain, aligning with the “Made in China 2025” vision.
  • Significance: Securing AI chips is pivotal for military, surveillance, and economic competitiveness. The espionage effort threatens global supply‑chain integrity, raises concerns about intellectual‑property theft, and deepens the strategic technology divide between the two powers.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Legal: Cross‑border espionage contravenes international law and bilateral agreements.
    • Ethical: Theft of proprietary designs undermines trust in multinational R&D collaborations.
    • Policy: Existing export‑control mechanisms may be insufficient to monitor covert acquisition channels such as front‑companies and academic collaborations.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen the Export Control Regime (ECR) by expanding the Strategic Goods List to include next‑generation AI chips.

    • Enhance cyber‑intelligence coordination among ministries (MEITY, DIPP, Ministry of Defence) to detect and counter foreign technology espionage.
