MP seeks action on accident-prone roads, railway overbridges in Ballari

22 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: A Member of Parliament, while chairing a District Development Coordination and Monitoring Committee (DDCMC) meeting in Ballari, demanded immediate action on roads and railway over‑bridges identified as accident‑prone. The call underscores the need for swift remedial measures to curb road‑traffic fatalities and improve infrastructure safety.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Public administration, infrastructure development, inter‑governmental coordination); GS Paper III – Technology & Economic Development (Road safety, transport infrastructure).

Key Data & Stats: District Development Coordination and Monitoring Committee (DDCMC) Ballari district, Karnataka

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: India records >150,000 road‑traffic deaths annually; Karnataka’s Ballari district has a higher-than‑average incidence of accidents on certain state highways and railway over‑bridges, often linked to poor maintenance, inadequate signage, and design flaws.
  • Significance: Safe road and rail infrastructure is a core component of the “Right to Life” under Article 21 of the Constitution. Reducing accident‑prone stretches directly impacts public health, economic productivity, and the credibility of local governance.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Fragmented responsibility among Public Works Department (PWD), Karnataka Road Development Corporation (KRDC), and Indian Railways leads to delays.
    • Limited budgetary allocation for routine audits and retro‑fitting of over‑bridges.
    • Absence of a systematic Road Safety Audit (RSA) mechanism at the district level.
  • Way Forward: • Institutionalise regular RSA for all identified high‑risk stretches under the aegis of the DDCMC.
    • Form a joint technical committee comprising PWD, KRDC, Indian Railways and the State Road Safety Cell (as per the National Road Safety Policy, 2019).
    • Mobilise funds through the Centrally Sponsored Scheme “Road Safety Infrastructure” and leverage PPP models for over‑bridge upgrades.
    • Adopt recommendations of the ARC on Road Safety (2015) – e.g., stricter enforcement of design standards, real‑time monitoring using GIS, and community‑based reporting mechanisms.

Prelims Pointers

  • District Development Coordination and Monitoring Committee (DDCMC) – a statutory platform for inter‑departmental coordination on district‑level projects.
  • Road Safety Audit (RSA) – mandated under the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways guidelines for high‑risk road sections.
  • National Road Safety Policy, 2019 – outlines the framework for reducing road‑traffic fatalities.
  • Article 21 of the Constitution – “Right to life and personal liberty” includes safety of citizens in public spaces.
