Karnataka plans to start two new medical colleges in Ramangara and Puttur next academic year

22 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Karnataka’s Medical Education Ministry announced plans to establish two new medical colleges at Ramangara and Puttur, targeting commencement in the 2027‑28 academic year. The state will seek approval from the National Medical Commission (NMC) for a minimum intake of 50 seats per college, aiming to augment regional health infrastructure and address doctor‑shortage gaps.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (State‑level health policy, statutory bodies); GS Paper III – Health and Family Welfare (Medical education, health infrastructure).

Key Data & Stats: 2 new colleges | Ramangara, Puttur (Karnataka) | 50 seats each | Application to NMC for 2027‑28 intake

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The National Medical Commission replaced the Medical Council of India in 2020, tightening norms for establishing medical institutions. Karnataka, already home to 31 medical colleges, faces a doctor‑to‑population ratio below the WHO benchmark, prompting the state to expand seats under the National Health Policy‑2017 target of 1.5 % annual increase in medical seats.
  • Significance: Adding 100 MBBS seats will improve rural health service delivery, create employment, and contribute to the central government's goal of achieving a doctor‑population ratio of 1:1,000. It also reflects cooperative federalism, where states utilize constitutional competence under List II (Health) to complement national health objectives.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Compliance with NMC’s infrastructure, faculty‑student ratio, and hospital bed‑strength norms may delay approval.
    • Financing the capital outlay and ensuring sustainable operation in remote districts pose fiscal constraints.
    • Potential duplication of seats without parallel increase in teaching hospitals could affect quality of medical education.
  • Way Forward: • Conduct a feasibility study by the Karnataka State Health Infrastructure Committee (modeled on the Sarkaria Commission’s approach to inter‑governmental coordination).
    • Leverage central schemes such as the Pradhan Mantri Swasthya Suraksha Yojana (PMSSY) for funding teaching‑hospital upgrades.
    • Adopt a phased intake model, starting with 30 seats and scaling up after meeting NMC benchmarks.
    • Ensure transparent monitoring through the State Health Management Information System (HMIS) to track outcomes.

Prelims Pointers

  • National Medical Commission (NMC) – statutory body governing medical education.
  • Health is a State List subject under Article 246(2) of the Constitution.
  • Doctor‑population ratio target: 1:1,000 (WHO norm).
  • Karnataka’s existing medical college count: 31 (as of 2023).
