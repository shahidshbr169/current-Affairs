SP, Congress kick off seat-sharing talks for U.P. polls

23 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Samajwadi Party (SP) and the Indian National Congress have initiated the first round of seat‑sharing negotiations for the Uttar Pradesh Legislative Assembly elections. The talks aim to allocate the 403 assembly constituencies between the two parties, a process that could shape the composition of the next state government.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Polity & Governance (Political parties, coalition politics, electoral reforms, constitutional provisions relating to elections and state governments).

Key Data & Stats: 403 assembly seats; first round of negotiations (no specific date provided).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Uttar Pradesh, with the largest electorate in India, has traditionally seen multi‑cornered contests. In the 2022 assembly elections, the SP emerged as the principal opposition to the BJP. Post‑2022, the SP and Congress have explored a pre‑poll alliance to consolidate anti‑incumbency votes, echoing earlier coalition attempts at the national level.
  • Significance: A successful seat‑sharing pact could alter the balance of power in the state legislature, influencing policy priorities such as law‑and‑order, agrarian reforms, and welfare schemes. It also tests the robustness of coalition politics within a federal structure, affecting centre‑state dynamics and the implementation of centrally sponsored programmes.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Reconciling divergent vote‑bank calculations and ideological stances of SP and Congress.
    • Ensuring compliance with the anti‑defection law (Tenth Schedule) if post‑election alliances shift.
    • Potential intra‑party dissent that could weaken campaign discipline.
    • Election Commission’s monitoring of seat‑sharing agreements to prevent violation of the Model Code of Conduct.
  • Way Forward:
    • Adopt a transparent, data‑driven seat allocation formula based on past vote shares and demographic mapping.
    • Consider formation of a joint coordination committee to resolve intra‑alliance disputes, akin to mechanisms recommended by the ARC for coalition stability.
    • Engage the Election Commission early to register the alliance and obtain guidance on permissible coordination activities.
    • Strengthen internal party democracy to mitigate dissent, ensuring that the alliance does not trigger frequent floor‑crossings under the anti‑defection law.
