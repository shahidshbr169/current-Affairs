Hear pleas by deleted voters first, Supreme Court directs Bengal SIR tribunals

23 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Supreme Court has ordered the Election Commission of India to expand the number of SIR (Sitting for Immediate Redress) tribunals and shift them to central hubs in Kolkata, aiming to clear the mounting backlog of appeals filed by voters whose names were deleted from the electoral roll.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Electoral reforms, judicial review, statutory bodies)

Key Data & Stats: Pending appeals: Not disclosed

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The SIR tribunals were constituted under the 2019 amendment to the Representation of People Act, 1951, to provide a speedy mechanism for aggrieved voters to challenge deletion from electoral rolls. Limited tribunal strength and their dispersion across the state have led to a significant case backlog.
  • Significance: Timely disposal of deletion appeals safeguards the fundamental right to vote (Article 21 A) and upholds the credibility of the electoral process, a cornerstone of India’s democratic fabric.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Inadequate number of tribunals relative to the volume of appeals.
    • Geographic dispersion causing logistical delays for petitioners.
    • Procedural bottlenecks and lack of digital infrastructure hinder expeditious hearings.
    • Potential overlap of jurisdiction between tribunals and higher courts.
  • Way Forward: • Increase tribunal strength as directed and locate them in major urban hubs for better accessibility.
    • Introduce virtual hearings and a case‑management portal to streamline filings.
    • Periodic review by an independent committee (e.g., a high‑level committee under the Election Commission) to monitor pendency.
    • Align tribunal procedures with the principles of natural justice and ensure adequate staffing.

Prelims Pointers

  • Supreme Court’s direction to the Election Commission of India (ECI).
  • SIR tribunals – established under the 2019 amendment to the Representation of People Act, 1951.
  • Fundamental right to vote – Article 21 A of the Constitution.
  • Electoral governance – Article 324 (Superintendence, direction and control of elections).
