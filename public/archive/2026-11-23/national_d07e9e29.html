What stands atop Thiruparankundram hill is a survey pillar, ascertains Tamil Nadu government report

23 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: A Tamil Nadu government report confirmed that the structure atop Thiruparankundram hill is a survey pillar, not a religious monument. This finding gained prominence after the Supreme Court allowed a one‑time lighting of the Karthigai Deepam lamp at a newly identified “deepathoon”, sparking a debate on heritage, religion and administrative authority.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; also relevant to GS Paper III – Security & Disaster Management (heritage conservation) and Ethics in Public Administration.

Key Data & Stats: Supreme Court judgment – limited, one‑time lighting permission Thiruparankundram hill – survey pillar identified

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Karthigai Deepam festival involves lighting a sacred lamp on hill‑tops. A long‑standing dispute existed over whether the structure on Thiruparankundram hill is a religious shrine or a technical survey pillar, leading to legal challenges and public protests.
  • Significance: The episode highlights the interface of religious freedom, heritage preservation, and state authority. It tests the limits of judicial intervention in cultural‑religious matters and underscores the need for clear demarcation of protected monuments under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act (AMASR Act).
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Balancing Article 25 (freedom of religion) with Article 21 (right to a wholesome environment) and heritage‑conservation statutes.
    • Absence of a definitive classification of the structure creates administrative ambiguity for the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department.
    • The Supreme Court’s “one‑time” order, while pragmatic, lacks a long‑term policy framework, risking future litigation.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a multi‑stakeholder committee (including the Archaeological Survey of India, State Heritage Department, and legal experts) to re‑examine the status of the hill‑top structure under the AMASR Act.
    • Issue clear guidelines for limited religious activities at non‑protected sites, possibly through an amendment to the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Act.
    • Adopt a periodic review mechanism, akin to the Sarkaria Commission’s recommendations on centre‑state coordination, to resolve similar heritage‑religion conflicts.

Prelims Pointers

  • Supreme Court judgment – limited, one‑time permission for Karthigai Deepam lighting.
  • Article 25 of the Constitution – freedom of religion.
  • Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act (AMASR Act) – classification of heritage structures.
  • Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Act – administrative control of temples and religious sites.
