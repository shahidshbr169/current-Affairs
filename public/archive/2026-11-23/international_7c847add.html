How did the COP31 climate agreement fall apart – and what happens now?

23 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: The COP31 climate summit in Sharm El‑Sheikh failed to produce a binding agreement, with the EU and several nations citing the process as inadequate amid escalating climate disasters and a looming breach of the 1.5 °C warming limit.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – International relations, global environmental groupings (UNFCCC, Paris Agreement), and multilateral climate diplomacy.

Key Data & Agreements: COP31 – Sharm El‑Sheikh, 2024 | Paris Agreement – 2015 | EU “Fit‑for‑55” target – 55 % emission cut by 2030

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The breakdown underscores the widening rift between developed and developing nations on finance, loss‑and‑damage, and mitigation commitments, potentially weakening the credibility of the UNFCCC framework and prompting alternative climate coalitions.
  • Impact on India: India faces heightened pressure to upscale its Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) while negotiating climate finance and technology transfer; domestic climate‑related disasters could strain fiscal resources and affect energy security.
  • Way Forward: India should champion a pragmatic “Common‑but‑Differentiated‑Responsibilities‑plus” approach, leverage the G20 and BRICS platforms for climate finance, and accelerate renewable energy targets under the National Solar Mission to hedge against global policy volatility.

Prelims Pointers

  • UNFCCC – the umbrella treaty governing COP negotiations.
  • Paris Agreement’s goal to limit warming to 1.5 °C above pre‑industrial levels.
  • COP31 held in Sharm El‑Sheikh, Egypt, 2024.
  • EU’s “Fit‑for‑55” package aims for a 55 % reduction in greenhouse gases by 2030.
