SC-ordered mediation fails in Sunjay Kapur’s Rs 30,000-crore family trust dispute

26 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Supreme Court terminated a court‑ordered mediation between Rani Kapur (mother of the late industrialist Sunjay Kapur) and Priya Sachdev Kapur (his widow) after 15 sessions led by former Chief Justice of India D.Y. Chandrachud failed to resolve a dispute over the validity of the RK Family Trust, which holds assets worth Rs 30,000 crore. The closure underscores the limits of judicial mediation in high‑value family‑trust litigations.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; Judicial Processes; Trusts & Corporate Governance.

Key Data & Stats: Rs 30,000 crore (value of RK Family Trust), 15 sessions (mediation attempts), Former CJI D.Y. Chandrachud (mediator).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The RK Family Trust was created by the late Sunjay Kapur to manage his industrial empire. After his demise, a succession dispute arose between his mother and widow, prompting the Supreme Court to order mediation under its powers to facilitate amicable settlement of civil disputes.
  • Significance: The case highlights the role of the Supreme Court in promoting alternative dispute resolution (ADR) for complex civil matters, the legal intricacies of family trusts under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, and the potential impact on corporate governance when large family‑owned conglomerates face internal conflicts.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Ambiguity in the statutory framework governing private family trusts, especially concerning valuation and succession rights.
    • Limited enforceability of mediation outcomes when parties remain entrenched, raising questions about the efficacy of court‑ordered mediation in high‑stakes disputes.
    • Potential conflict of interest when a former CJI acts as mediator, necessitating clear guidelines on post‑retirement judicial involvement in ADR.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitution of a specialised “Family Trust and Succession Tribunal” (as recommended by the Law Commission) to adjudicate large private trusts with clear procedural rules.
    • Amendment to the Indian Trusts Act to incorporate explicit provisions on valuation, beneficiary rights, and mandatory arbitration clauses for intra‑family disputes.
    • Formulation of guidelines by the Supreme Court or a committee (e.g., an ARC‑type committee) on the appointment of retired judges as mediators to ensure transparency and avoid perceived bias.
    • Encourage parties to adopt pre‑emptive dispute‑resolution clauses in trust deeds, including arbitration and mediation, to reduce judicial burden.

Prelims Pointers

  • Supreme Court’s power to order mediation under Article 141 (law declared by the Supreme Court) and its role in civil ADR.
  • Indian Trusts Act, 1882 – applicability to private family trusts and gaps in succession provisions.
  • Role of former Chief Justices in mediation – need for statutory guidelines.
  • Concept of “Family Trust” and its relevance to corporate governance and inheritance law.
