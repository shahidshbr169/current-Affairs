Parliament To Reconvene On Nov 30 Amid Delimitation-Women's Quota Bill Buzz

26 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Lok Sabha will reconvene on 30 Nov 2026 to debate the Delimitation‑Women’s Quota Bill. With three seats vacant, the government must secure 361 votes to achieve the special majority required for a constitutional amendment.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Articles 368, 324); GS Paper I – Current Affairs (Parliamentary Procedure, Delimitation Commission).

Key Data & Stats: 30 Nov 2026 – Parliament reconvenes, 3 vacant Lok Sabha seats, 361 votes needed for special majority, 108 seats reserved for women (proposed).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Women’s Reservation Bill, first introduced in 1996, seeks to reserve 33 % of seats for women in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. Its recent revival is tied to the pending delimitation exercise, which will redraw constituency boundaries after the 2026 census, providing an opportunity to embed the quota.
  • Significance: Passage would amend the Constitution under Article 368, altering the composition of the lower house and potentially enhancing gender parity in legislative decision‑making. It also tests the ruling party’s ability to marshal a special majority, reflecting on parliamentary stability and coalition dynamics.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Achieving 361 votes requires cross‑party consensus; opposition parties have raised concerns about the impact on merit‑based elections and the timing with delimitation. Legal challenges may invoke the “basic structure” doctrine if the quota is perceived to alter the federal balance. Additionally, the Delimitation Commission’s independence could be questioned if political considerations dominate the redrawing of constituencies.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a bipartisan committee (modeled on the Joint Parliamentary Committee) to examine the operational aspects of women’s reservation and delimitation.
    • Seek advisory input from the Election Commission of India and the Law Commission on constitutional safeguards.
    • Consider phased implementation – first in the Lok Sabha, followed by State Assemblies – to manage political resistance.
    • Monitor compliance through the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Law & Justice post‑enactment.

Prelims Pointers

  • Special majority for constitutional amendment: >50 % of total Lok Sabha strength + two‑thirds of members present and voting (Article 368).
  • Delimitation Commission – constitutional body under Article 324 responsible for redrawing constituencies after each census.
  • Women’s Reservation Bill proposes reservation of 33 % (108 seats) for women in Lok Sabha.
  • Current Lok Sabha strength: 543 seats; with 3 vacancies, total effective strength is 540.
