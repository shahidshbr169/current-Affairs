Special Parliament sitting from November 30 likely to take up amended Delimitation Bill

26 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Union Cabinet is slated to meet on 27 Nov 2024 to approve an amended Delimitation Bill, after which Parliament will convene a special sitting from 30 Nov 2024 to discuss the legislation. The move seeks to update constituency boundaries ahead of the next census, impacting electoral representation across states.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; Electoral Reforms; Federal Structure; Articles 82 & 170 of the Constitution.

Key Data & Stats: 30 Nov 2024 – Special Parliamentary sitting, 27 Nov 2024 – Cabinet meeting, 2026 – Current freeze on delimitation till after the 2026 census

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Delimitation Commission, constituted under Article 82, last redrew constituencies based on the 2001 Census. A constitutional amendment froze delimitation until after the 2026 Census to preserve state‑wise seat allocation. The amended Bill proposes a framework to resume delimitation post‑2026, addressing demographic shifts and urban‑rural imbalances.
  • Significance: Timely delimitation ensures “one‑person‑one‑vote” equity, corrects over‑ or under‑representation, and can alter the political arithmetic in Parliament and State Assemblies. It also impacts the allocation of seats for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and women (if linked to reservation reforms).
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Potential legal challenges under Article 170 (distribution of seats among states) and the 84th Amendment’s freeze provisions.
    • Political resistance from states fearing loss of seats due to slower population growth.
    • Administrative bottlenecks in acquiring accurate post‑census data and ensuring transparent delimitation criteria.
  • Way Forward:
    • Set up an expert committee (e.g., a high‑level Delimitation Review Committee) with representation from the Election Commission, Ministry of Law & Justice, and state governments.
    • Adopt the Sarkaria Commission’s principle of “balanced federalism” while revising seat allocation.
    • Ensure public consultation and judicial oversight to pre‑empt litigation.
    • Link delimitation with broader electoral reforms such as the introduction of VVPAT‑enabled EVMs and strengthening the Model Code of Conduct.

Prelims Pointers

  • Delimitation Bill – amendment to resume constituency re‑organisation post‑2026 census.
  • Constitutional Articles 82 & 170 – provisions for delimitation and allocation of seats.
  • Delimitation Commission – independent body headed by a retired Supreme Court judge.
  • Freeze on delimitation – instituted by the 84th Amendment (2001) until after the 2026 Census.
