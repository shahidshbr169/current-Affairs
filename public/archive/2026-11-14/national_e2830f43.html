Will R&AW’s overseas operations affect ties? | Explained

14 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The United States, Canada, Australia and Pakistan have alleged that India’s external intelligence agency, R&AW, has carried out targeted killings and surveillance of separatist elements on their territories. The Indian government maintains that its overseas operations are guided by international law and are aimed at safeguarding national security, prompting a debate on the legal and diplomatic norms governing covert actions in friendly states.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Security, Law & International Relations

Key Data & Stats: 2024 US, Canada, Australia, Pakistan – allegations Intelligence Agencies (Amendment) Act, 2008 UN Charter Art. 2(4) – non‑intervention

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: R&AW, created in 1968, operates under the Intelligence Agencies (Amendment) Act, 2008. Over the past decade, India has faced heightened separatist movements abroad (e.g., Khalistani, Kashmiri, and Baloch groups). In response, intelligence agencies have intensified cross‑border monitoring, leading to the current allegations of covert operations.
  • Significance: The issue touches upon the delicate balance between a sovereign state’s right to protect its internal security (Article 355 of the Constitution) and the principles of non‑intervention and respect for territorial sovereignty under international law. Repeated allegations can strain diplomatic ties, affect strategic partnerships, and invite reciprocal actions.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Legal ambiguity – India lacks a comprehensive statutory framework governing covert overseas actions, relying largely on executive discretion.
    • Accountability – No parliamentary oversight mechanism specifically for external intelligence, raising concerns about democratic control.
    • Ethical dilemmas – Targeted killings and surveillance may contravene human‑rights norms and the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations if diplomatic cover is misused.
    • Diplomatic fallout – Accusations from friendly nations can lead to reciprocal surveillance, expulsion of diplomats, or sanctions.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a Parliamentary Committee on External Intelligence (modeled on the Committee on Public Undertakings) to review operational guidelines and ensure compliance with international law.
    • Draft a “Covert Operations Protocol” incorporating the principles of necessity, proportionality, and respect for host‑state sovereignty, referencing UN Charter Art. 2(4) and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.
    • Strengthen diplomatic channels – Institutionalise a “Strategic Liaison Desk” within the Ministry of External Affairs to pre‑emptively communicate legitimate security concerns to partner countries.
    • Periodic audit by the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) on the financial and operational aspects of overseas missions to enhance transparency.

Prelims Pointers

  • R&AW operates under the Intelligence Agencies (Amendment) Act, 2008.
  • Article 355 of the Indian Constitution mandates the Union to protect the nation against internal and external threats.
  • UN Charter Article 2(4) enshrines the principle of non‑intervention in the internal affairs of sovereign states.
  • Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) governs diplomatic immunity and the permissible use of diplomatic premises for intelligence activities.
