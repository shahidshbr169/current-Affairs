Women must get adequate representation in Parliament, legislatures: Karnataka CM D.K. Shivakumar

14 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Karnataka Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar pledged full governmental support to the Commonwealth Women Parliamentarians’ India‑region conference in Bengaluru, urging that women attain adequate representation in Parliament and state legislatures. The statement underscores the push for gender‑balanced law‑making as a governance priority.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper I – Women & Child Development; Ethics (values of gender equity)

Key Data & Stats: Article 15(3) Women in Lok Sabha: ~14% Women in Raj Sabha: ~11% Commonwealth Women Parliamentarians (CWP) – 30+ member nations

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: India’s women’s representation in elected bodies remains among the lowest in the world despite constitutional guarantee under Article 15(3) for equality of opportunity. The Commonwealth Women Parliamentarians (CWP) forum, established in 1989, convenes annually to promote gender parity in legislatures across member nations. Karnataka’s hosting of the India‑region conference reflects the state’s recent emphasis on women‑centric policies.
  • Significance: Enhancing women’s presence in Parliament improves policy responsiveness to gender‑specific issues (health, education, safety) and aligns with SDG‑5 and the UN’s “Women’s Political Empowerment” agenda. Government backing signals political will, potentially catalysing legislative reforms such as a statutory reservation for women at the national level.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Absence of a constitutional or statutory quota for women in Parliament; reliance on voluntary party tickets limits scale. Patriarchal party structures, electoral financing constraints, and sociocultural barriers impede women candidates. Legal challenge: any amendment for reservation must navigate the basic structure doctrine and may require a constitutional amendment under Article 368.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute a high‑level committee (e.g., under the Ministry of Women & Child Development) to examine a 33% reservation model, drawing on the 73rd/74th Constitutional Amendments as precedent. • Encourage political parties to adopt internal quotas (as recommended by the Election Commission). • Strengthen capacity‑building programmes for prospective women legislators (training, mentorship). • Monitor progress through an annual “Women Representation Index” reported to Parliament.

Prelims Pointers

  • Constitutional provision – Article 15(3) – equality of opportunity for women.
  • Current representation – Lok Sabha ~14%, Raj Sabha ~11%.
  • Commonwealth Women Parliamentarians (CWP) – platform for gender‑balanced legislation across Commonwealth nations.
  • Potential legislative route – constitutional amendment under Article 368 for a statutory reservation of women in Parliament.
