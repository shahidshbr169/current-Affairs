U.S. media names Indian official who allegedly ordered plot on Gurpatwant Singh Pannun

14 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: A Washington Post investigation alleges that RAW officer Vikash Yadav instructed businessman Nikhil Gupta, now detained in the Czech Republic, to contract a hit‑man for the assassination of Sikh activist Gurpatwant Singh Pannun in New York. The report further suggests that NSA Ajit Doval may have been aware of the plot, raising serious questions about oversight of India’s intelligence agencies.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Internal Security, Law and Justice; Ethics in Public Administration.

Key Data & Stats: Vikash Yadav – RAW officer Nikhil Gupta – in Czech custody Target: Gurpatwant Singh Pannun (NY residence) Source: Washington Post investigation

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: RAW’s mandate under the Official Secrets Act, 1923 and the National Security Act, 1980 is limited to external intelligence. Allegations of extraterritorial covert operations against dissidents have surfaced intermittently, prompting debates on the agency’s jurisdiction and accountability.
  • Significance: If substantiated, the episode implicates the highest echelons of India’s security establishment, potentially breaching international law, diplomatic norms, and constitutional safeguards such as Article 21 (right to life) and Article 22 (procedure established by law). It also risks diplomatic friction with the United States and the Czech Republic.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Lack of statutory oversight mechanisms for RAW; ambiguity in the role of the NSA vis‑à‑vis operational decisions; potential conflict with the principle of “no extrajudicial killings” as reiterated in Supreme Court judgments (e.g., People’s Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India, 2003). The incident also highlights the difficulty of prosecuting covert actions that occur abroad.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute a parliamentary committee (e.g., a joint committee of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) to examine the chain of command and oversight gaps in intelligence operations.
    • Strengthen the role of the Intelligence Oversight and Review Committee (proposed under the National Security Act) to ensure periodic audit of covert actions.
    • Align RAW’s operational guidelines with international human‑rights standards and seek clarification from the Supreme Court on the applicability of Article 21 to covert overseas actions.
    • Consider a review by the Sarkaria Commission‑type body on centre‑state coordination in security matters, given the potential impact on India’s diplomatic posture.

Prelims Pointers

  • RAW – Research and Analysis Wing (External intelligence agency)
  • NSA – National Security Advisor (chief coordinator of security and foreign policy)
  • Article 21, Constitution of India – Right to life and personal liberty
  • Supreme Court judgment: People’s Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2003) – prohibition of extrajudicial killings
