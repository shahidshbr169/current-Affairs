'How were 2024 elections legitimate?' Rahul accuses EC of declaring 140 million who voted illegal

14 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Rahul Gandhi questioned the legitimacy of the 2024 Lok Sabha elections, alleging that the Election Commission classified about 140 million voters as “non‑existent or non‑voters”. The claim raises concerns over the credibility of the electoral roll and the functioning of the Election Commission.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Election Commission, Article 324, Representation of People Act, 1951); GS Paper III – Security & Internal Disturbances (implications of perceived electoral illegitimacy).

Key Data & Stats: 140 million voters alleged as non‑existent / non‑voters 2024 Lok Sabha elections

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Election Commission (EC) maintains the electoral roll under Article 324 of the Constitution and the Representation of People Act, 1951. Periodic revisions aim to delete deceased or duplicate entries, but large‑scale deletions often attract scrutiny, especially in a high‑stakes general election.
  • Significance: Trust in the EC is pivotal for the legitimacy of democratic processes. Allegations of mass disenfranchisement can fuel public dissent, affect voter turnout, and invite judicial review, thereby impacting the stability of the elected government.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Verification mechanisms: Reliance on outdated voter lists, inadequate integration of Aadhaar‑based authentication, and limited real‑time updates can create gaps.
    • Legal safeguards: The EC’s decisions are subject to judicial scrutiny under Articles 324 and 324(1), but the lack of transparent criteria for “non‑existent” classification hampers accountability.
    • Political perception: Even if procedurally correct, large‑scale deletions may be perceived as partisan, eroding the EC’s constitutional independence.
  • Way Forward:
    • Establish an independent audit committee (e.g., under the ARC framework) to periodically review the methodology of voter deletion.
    • Mandate real‑time linkage of the electoral roll with the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) to minimize duplication.
    • Introduce a statutory grievance redressal mechanism with a stipulated timeline (< 30 days) for aggrieved voters to contest deletion.
    • Periodic parliamentary oversight through a standing committee on Election Affairs to ensure transparency and public confidence.

Prelims Pointers

  • Election Commission of India – constitutional body under Article 324.
  • Representation of People Act, 1951 – governs preparation and revision of electoral rolls.
  • Voter‑verified paper audit trail (VVPAT) – tool for post‑poll verification.
  • Article 324(1) – EC’s power to supervise, direct, and control elections.
