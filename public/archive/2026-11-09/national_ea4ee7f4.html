Delhi air pollution: Health experts warn of health risks as even healthy people report symptoms

9 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Doctors from AIIMS and Apollo Hospitals have reported a surge in respiratory ailments among otherwise healthy Delhi residents, attributing the spike to the city’s severe air‑pollution levels. They caution that even “green” firecrackers provide only limited relief, underscoring the broader public‑health threat posed by persistent poor air quality.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment & Ecology; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Public Health & Policy)

Key Data & Stats: young adults aged 20‑50 reporting symptoms, green crackers only partially mitigate emissions

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Delhi’s AQI regularly breaches the National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) for PM2.5 (60 µg/m³ annual). The National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) 2019‑2024 aims for a 20‑30% reduction in PM levels, but implementation gaps persist, especially during winter festivals when firecracker usage spikes.
  • Significance: Air‑pollution‑induced morbidity strains the public‑health system, raises healthcare costs, and hampers productivity. The episode highlights the need for stricter enforcement of emission norms and for integrating health impact assessments into urban planning.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Inadequate real‑time monitoring and data sharing across agencies.
    • Legal ambiguity over “green” firecrackers – they meet emission standards but still emit toxic metals.
    • Enforcement of the Supreme Court’s M.C. Mehta vs. Union of India (2006) directives on vehicular and industrial emissions remains uneven.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen the role of the National Green Tribunal (NGT) to fast‑track violations related to firecrackers.
    • Constitute a multi‑sectoral task‑force (MoEFCC, MoHFW, Delhi Pollution Control Committee) to audit health outcomes of air‑quality interventions.
    • Promote low‑emission transport (electric buses, CNG) and expand the “odd‑even” scheme during high‑pollution periods.
    • Encourage community‑level awareness campaigns on indoor air‑purifiers and use of N95 masks during peak episodes.

Prelims Pointers

  • National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) – target 20‑30% reduction in PM2.5 by 2024.
  • Supreme Court judgment: M.C. Mehta vs. Union of India (2006) – “polluter pays” principle.
  • National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) – PM2.5 limit: 60 µg/m³ (annual).
  • Key statutory bodies: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), Delhi Pollution Control Committee (DPCC), National Green Tribunal (NGT).
