UPSC Key-9th Nov 2026: Siddis of Gujarat, Net Zero and Multidimensional Poverty

9 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The UPSC Key for 9 Nov 2026 highlights three thematic areas – the socio‑economic status of the Siddis community in Gujarat, India’s commitment to net‑zero emissions, and the measurement of multidimensional poverty using the World Bank’s India Poverty and Equity Brief. These topics are flagged as high‑yield for both prelims and mains because they intersect policy, data‑driven assessment, and international benchmarks.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Technology, Environment, Sustainable Development; GS Paper IV – Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude (social inclusion).

Key Data & Stats: World Bank India Poverty and Equity Brief (2026) Siddis – Scheduled Tribe status, Gujarat (population ~ 1.2 lakh) India’s Net‑Zero target: 2070 Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) – 2024: 0.119

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Siddis, an Afro‑Indian community, have long sought greater socio‑economic uplift. Simultaneously, India’s climate commitments and the World Bank’s multidimensional poverty framework have gained prominence in policy circles.
  • Significance: Understanding the Siddis’ status tests knowledge of tribal welfare schemes (e.g., TRIFED, PESA). Net‑zero discussions link to the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act and the Nationally Determined Contributions. The MPI approach shifts focus from income‑based poverty to health, education, and living standards, aligning with SDG‑1.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Implementation gaps in tribal development, inter‑state coordination, and data reliability for MPI. Legal challenges may arise in reconciling existing environmental clearances with net‑zero pathways. Ethical concerns about categorising communities without adequate participatory input.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute a high‑level committee (modeled on the NITI‑Aayog “Tribal Development Task Force”) to integrate Siddis’ data into the national poverty dashboard.
    • Accelerate renewable energy procurement under the “Green Energy Corridor” to meet the 2070 net‑zero timeline.
    • Adopt the World Bank’s MPI methodology in the Periodic Labour Force Survey for consistent tracking.
    • Periodic review by the ARC on the impact of climate‑linked policies on vulnerable groups.

Prelims Pointers

  • World Bank India Poverty and Equity Brief – multidimensional poverty measurement.
  • Siddis of Gujarat – recognized as a Scheduled Tribe under the Constitution (Article 342).
  • India’s Net‑Zero target – pledged for 2070 under the Paris Agreement.
  • Technology Readiness Levels (TRL) – used to assess readiness of clean‑energy technologies for large‑scale deployment.
