KMC Commissioner directs officials to identify dumping spots, boost awareness on waste disposal

9 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Kurnool Municipal Commissioner has instructed officials to demarcate new dumping sites and intensify public awareness on proper waste disposal. He also mandated that solid‑waste collection continue unabated, even on public holidays, to prevent littering and health hazards.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance & Polity (Urban Local Bodies, Public Administration, 74th Constitutional Amendment); GS Paper III – Environment & Ecology (Solid Waste Management Rules, Swachh Bharat Mission).

Key Data & Stats: Kurnool Municipal Corporation (KMC) Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Rapid urbanisation and inadequate segregation have led to mounting solid‑waste volumes in tier‑2 cities like Kurnool. Previous gaps in collection on holidays often resulted in illegal dumping and public health concerns.
  • Significance: Continuous waste collection safeguards public health, curbs vector‑borne diseases, and aligns with the Swachh Bharat Mission’s target of “Open Defecation Free” and “Clean Cities”. It also reflects the municipal duty under Article 243W (74th Amendment) to provide essential services.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Limited landfill capacity and scarcity of scientifically sanctioned dumping sites.
    • Low citizen participation in segregation and awareness programmes.
    • Financial constraints for upgrading collection fleet and setting up decentralized processing units.
    • Enforcement gaps of the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016.
  • Way Forward: • Form a municipal‑level “Solid Waste Management Committee” as envisaged in the 2016 Rules to monitor compliance.
    • Adopt decentralized composting and waste‑to‑energy models (ARC‑recommended for Tier‑2 cities).
    • Mobilise funds under the AMRUT and Swachh Bharat Mission for additional collection vehicles and GIS‑based monitoring.
    • Conduct regular IEC (Information, Education, Communication) drives in schools and colonies; involve NGOs for community‑based segregation.
    • Periodic audit by State Pollution Control Board to ensure environmental compliance.

Prelims Pointers

  • Article 243W, 74th Constitutional Amendment – duties of Urban Local Bodies.
  • Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 – provisions for segregation, collection, processing, and disposal.
  • Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) – target of 100% door‑to‑door waste collection.
  • Kurnool Municipal Corporation – example of municipal initiative to ensure uninterrupted waste services.
