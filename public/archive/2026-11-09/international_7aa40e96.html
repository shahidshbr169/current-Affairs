Bangladesh Foreign Ministry examining Sheikh Hasina’s request for new passport

9 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: Bangladesh’s Foreign Ministry is reviewing former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina’s request for a new passport after her travel document was cancelled following her flight to India in August 2024 amid a sudden political upheaval.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral relations, regional groupings (SAARC, BIMSTEC), and India’s diplomatic engagement with neighbouring countries.

Key Data & Agreements: August 2024 – passport cancellation, Bangladesh‑India land border (4,096 km), SAARC charter (Article 2 – peaceful coexistence)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The episode underscores the fragility of political transitions in South Asia and tests the norms of diplomatic asylum, potentially influencing regional power equations and the credibility of Bangladesh’s democratic institutions.
  • Impact on India: India faces a delicate balancing act—providing humanitarian assistance to a former head of state while managing bilateral sensitivities, border security, and its image as a responsible regional actor.
  • Way Forward: India should engage through back‑channel diplomacy, reaffirm its commitment to non‑interference, and leverage SAARC/BIMSTEC mechanisms to encourage a peaceful resolution and safeguard border stability.

Prelims Pointers

  • Bangladesh‑India bilateral ties are anchored in the 1996 Land Boundary Agreement and regular diplomatic consultations.
  • SAARC and BIMSTEC provide platforms for addressing cross‑border political crises and humanitarian issues.
  • International law on diplomatic asylum is not uniformly codified; the 1954 Convention on Diplomatic Asylum (not ratified by India) offers limited guidance.
  • Any escalation could affect regional projects such as the Bangladesh‑India gas pipeline and trade corridors.
