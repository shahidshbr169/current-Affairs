Our future must be shaped by ‘cooperation, not competition’: PM Modi to ASEAN leaders

17 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: At the India‑ASEAN summit, Prime Minister Narendra Modi emphasized that the future of the region should be built on “co‑operation, not competition” and reaffirmed India’s unwavering commitment to ASEAN’s unity and centrality in the partnership.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; India‑ASEAN relations; regional cooperation frameworks.

Key Data & Agreements: India‑ASEAN Summit, 2024

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The statement underscores India’s strategic intent to position itself as a balancing force in Indo‑Pacific geopolitics, countering polarising narratives and promoting a rules‑based order that favours multilateral cooperation.
  • Impact on India: Reinforces India’s diplomatic leverage in securing maritime security cooperation, trade linkages, and technology partnerships with ASEAN members, while bolstering its credibility as a “net‑security provider” in the region.
  • Way Forward: India should deepen sectoral dialogues (e.g., supply‑chain resilience, renewable energy, digital connectivity), institutionalise joint exercises under the ASEAN‑India Strategic Partnership, and champion inclusive mechanisms within the Quad and East Asia Summit to sustain cooperative momentum.

Prelims Pointers

  • India‑ASEAN Strategic Partnership (established 1992) – a cornerstone of India’s Act East Policy.
  • ASEAN’s “centrality” principle guides regional architecture, influencing initiatives like the Indo‑Pacific Outlook.
  • Key forums linking India and ASEAN: BIMSTEC, East Asia Summit, and the Quad (as a complementary platform).
  • Co‑operation focus areas: maritime security, connectivity (e.g., India‑Myanmar Trilateral Highway), and climate‑resilient development.
