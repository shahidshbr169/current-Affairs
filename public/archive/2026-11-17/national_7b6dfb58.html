Temperature drops in Munnar, 6℃ recorded on Tuesday

17 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: On Tuesday, temperature in the Munnar tea‑growing belt fell to a record low of 6 °C, with nearby stations Chundavurai, Sevenmalai and Devikulam recording 7 °C and Letchmi and R&D Mattuppetty at 8 °C. The abrupt cold snap threatens tea yield, tourism, and local livelihoods, prompting a review of climate‑resilience measures in the high‑altitude agrarian zones of Kerala.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment & Ecology; GS Paper II – Governance (Disaster Management, Agricultural Policy); GS Paper I – Geography (Climatic Zones of India).

Key Data & Stats: 6 °C – lowest temperature recorded in Munnar on Tuesday, 7 °C – recorded at Chundavurai, Sevenmalai, Devikulam, 8 °C – recorded at Letchmi, R&D Mattuppetty, Munnar – major tea‑producing region (≈ 30 % of Kerala’s tea output).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Western Ghats experience high diurnal temperature variation; recent anomalous cold spells are linked to altered jet‑stream patterns and delayed onset of the southwest monsoon. UPASI’s continuous monitoring provides real‑time data for agronomic interventions.
  • Significance: Tea buds are highly sensitive to frost; temperatures below 7 °C can cause bud‑kill, reducing annual yield by up to 10‑15 %. The tourism sector, a secondary income source, also suffers as trekking and wildlife safaris become unsafe.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Inadequate cold‑weather early warning systems at the district level; limited access to frost‑resistant tea clones; insufficient insurance penetration under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) for high‑altitude crops; coordination gaps between Kerala State Disaster Management Authority (KSDMA) and agricultural extension services.
  • Way Forward:
    • Form a joint task‑force of the Ministry of Agriculture, Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change, and KSDMA to develop a “Cold‑Snap Response Protocol” for hill plantations.
    • Promote adoption of frost‑tolerant tea varieties through the National Agricultural Innovation Fund (NAIF).
    • Expand climate‑risk insurance coverage for tea growers, with premium subsidies for farms below 1500 m altitude.
    • Leverage the Inter‑State Council (per Article 263) to share best practices from Himachal Pradesh’s frost‑mitigation schemes.
    • Integrate real‑time temperature data from UPASI into the India Meteorological Department’s (IMD) early warning portal for farmers.

Prelims Pointers

  • Record low temperature in Munnar: 6 °C (Tuesday).
  • Key agencies: United Planters’ Association of South India (UPASI), Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers’ Welfare, Kerala State Disaster Management Authority.
  • Relevant constitutional provision: Article 48A – State’s duty to protect and improve environment.
  • Policy framework: National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) – “Sustainable Agriculture” mission; Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) for climate‑risk insurance.
