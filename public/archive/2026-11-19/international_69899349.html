India, Japan discuss economic security, deeper business, people-to-people ties

19 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: India and Japan held talks to deepen economic security cooperation, expand business linkages and strengthen people‑to‑people contacts. External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar warned against the “weaponisation” of connectivity, resources, services, supply chains and market shares, linking these trends to national security concerns.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; Economic security and strategic partnerships.

Key Data & Agreements: Economic Security Dialogue

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The dialogue underscores a shift from traditional defence‑centric ties to a broader economic‑security framework, reflecting the Quad’s emphasis on resilient supply chains and the contestation of critical infrastructure by major powers.
  • Impact on India: Enhances India’s strategic autonomy by diversifying supply sources, reducing dependence on any single market, and bolstering technological collaboration in sectors such as semiconductors, renewable energy and digital infrastructure.
  • Way Forward: Institutionalise a joint India‑Japan Economic Security Working Group; pursue co‑development of secure 5G/6G networks; expand SME‑level trade missions; and align with Quad initiatives on supply‑chain resilience.

Prelims Pointers

  • India‑Japan Strategic Partnership – upgraded to “Comprehensive Global Strategic Partnership” (2022) with focus on economic security.
  • Quad (USA, Japan, India, Australia) – collaborative platform for supply‑chain diversification and technology standards.
  • Weaponisation of connectivity – a growing concern highlighted by Jaishankar, relevant to debates on digital sovereignty.
  • People‑to‑people ties – increase in student exchanges and cultural programmes as soft‑power tools.
