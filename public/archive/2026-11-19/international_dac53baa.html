West Asia crisis 'largest threat' to energy security ever: IEA

19 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: The International Energy Agency (IEA) has warned that the ongoing crisis in West Asia poses the “largest threat” to global energy security ever, noting that consumers and soaring prices are now bearing the brunt as traditional buffers erode.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – International relations, energy security, and the impact of geopolitical crises on global oil markets.

Key Data & Agreements: IEA 2024 report

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The West Asian turmoil disrupts a region that supplies over 30% of global oil, threatening supply chains, inflating commodity prices and reshaping the strategic calculus of major powers.
  • Impact on India: India, as a net oil importer, faces heightened import bills, balance‑of‑payments pressure, and potential inflationary spill‑overs; energy‑intensive sectors may see reduced competitiveness.
  • Way Forward: Diversify energy mix (renewables, LNG), strengthen strategic petroleum reserves, pursue diplomatic engagement with OPEC+ and regional actors, and explore bilateral energy cooperation (e.g., with UAE, Saudi Arabia).

Prelims Pointers

  • IEA’s 2024 assessment flags West Asia crisis as the biggest threat to global energy security.
  • Region accounts for > 30% of world oil production; disruptions directly affect global oil prices.
  • India’s oil import dependence stands at ~ 80% of its total oil consumption.
  • Strategic options include expanding strategic petroleum reserves and accelerating renewable energy targets.
