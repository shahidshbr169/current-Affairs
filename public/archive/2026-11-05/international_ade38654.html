Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu's position weakens as ultra-Orthodox party withholds support

Core Summary: Israel’s parliamentary election held on 27 Oct 2024 ended in a stalemate, with neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu nor his rival Gadi Eisenkot able to secure a clear majority. The ultra‑Orthodox coalition’s refusal to back either camp has weakened Netanyahu’s position and ushered in a period of political uncertainty.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – International Relations: Bilateral and regional dynamics involving Israel, the Middle‑East, and India; implications for strategic groupings (Quad, G20) and security architecture.

Key Data & Agreements: Election deadlock – no party crossed the 61‑seat majority threshold

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The impasse hampers Israel’s ability to formulate decisive foreign‑policy moves, potentially slowing ongoing peace initiatives (e.g., Abraham Accords) and affecting the balance of power between Iran and its regional rivals. A pro‑Netanyahu government traditionally aligns closely with the United States, whereas a coalition led by centrist or left‑leaning forces could recalibrate Israel’s stance toward the Arab world.
  • Impact on India: India’s strategic partnership with Israel—spanning defence procurement, agriculture technology, and counter‑terrorism cooperation—may face delays in project approvals and joint exercises. The political uncertainty could also affect the safety of the sizable Indian diaspora and business community in Israel, and influence India’s diplomatic calculus in the broader Middle‑East, especially regarding energy security and the Iran‑Israel rivalry.
  • Way Forward: India should:
    • Maintain a balanced diplomatic engagement with all major Israeli political formations to safeguard defence and technology projects.
    • Strengthen multilateral channels (e.g., G20, UN) to advocate for a stable Israeli government that can uphold existing peace accords.
    • Enhance consular support and real‑time monitoring for Indian nationals in Israel, possibly via a joint India‑Israel task force.
    • Leverage the situation to deepen ties with other regional actors (UAE, Saudi Arabia) to ensure continuity of energy supplies and strategic cooperation.

Prelims Pointers

  • Israel’s election deadlock: no party achieved the 61‑seat majority required to form a government.
  • India‑Israel strategic partnership includes annual defence trade worth over $2 billion and cooperation in agriculture, water management, and cyber‑security.
  • Israel is a key member of the Quad‑like Indo‑Pacific strategic dialogue on technology and security, influencing India’s broader regional posture.
  • Potential impact on the Abraham Accords framework, which India supports as a means to enhance stability in the Middle East.
