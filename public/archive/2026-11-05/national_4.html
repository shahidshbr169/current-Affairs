Was Michael Burry right about the AI bubble? Wall Street loses $2 trillion as AI and chip stocks plunge: Why are Tesla, Palantir, AMD, Broadcom and SanDisk crashing?

Core Summary: A sharp correction in AI‑related equities erased over $2 trillion in market capitalisation on 5 Nov 2026, with major stocks such as AMD (‑12 %), SanDisk (‑14 %), Micron (‑10 %), Nvidia (‑7 %), Tesla (‑9 %) and Palantir (‑8 %) plunging sharply. The episode revives concerns raised by investor Michael Burry about an “AI bubble” and spotlights the need for robust market‑regulatory oversight of emerging‑technology valuations.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Economic Growth, Investment & Capital Markets; GS Paper I – Science & Technology (AI, semiconductor sector); GS Paper II – Institutions (SEBI, RBI, Ministry of Finance) and Governance of Financial Markets.

Key Data & Stats: $2 trillion market‑value loss; AMD –12 %; SanDisk –14 %; Micron –10 %; Nvidia –7 %; Tesla –9 %; Palantir –8 %; 5 Nov 2026

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Post‑pandemic fiscal stimulus, low‑interest rates and aggressive venture funding propelled AI‑driven startups and chip manufacturers. Parallelly, speculative inflows, high‑frequency trading and retail enthusiasm inflated valuations beyond fundamentals, prompting warnings from market veterans like Michael Burry.
  • Significance: The correction tests the resilience of India’s capital markets, raises questions on investor protection, and underscores the importance of a calibrated AI/technology policy that balances innovation incentives with systemic risk mitigation.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Lack of sector‑specific regulatory guidelines for AI‑related securities.
    • Potential contagion risk to Indian listed AI‑chip firms (e.g., Tata Elxsi, HCL‑Tech).
    • Information asymmetry: retail investors often lack deep technical insight into AI valuation metrics.
    • Ethical concerns over hype‑driven AI deployments without adequate governance.
  • Way Forward: • SEBI to issue a “Technology‑Sector Disclosure Framework” mandating detailed AI‑risk and valuation disclosures.
    • RBI to monitor systemic risk via stress‑testing of AI‑exposed financial institutions.
    • Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology (MeitY) to convene an expert committee (modeled on the NITI‑Aayog AI Task Force) for periodic review of AI market dynamics.
    • Encourage adoption of International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) for intangible AI assets to improve comparability.

Prelims Pointers

  • SEBI – Securities and Exchange Board of India: regulator of stock‑market disclosures and market‑manipulation.
  • RBI – Role in systemic risk assessment of technology‑exposed financial entities.
  • AI policy framework – NITI‑Aayog’s “National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence” (2018) and its relevance to market regulation.
  • International best‑practice: U.S. SEC’s “Risk Disclosures for Emerging Technologies” as a reference model.
