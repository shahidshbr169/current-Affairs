Parliament's Monsoon session remains unprorogued for 100 days, government yet to announce special sitting

21 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Monsoon session of Parliament was adjourned sine die on 13 Aug 2024 but has not been prorogued for over 100 days as of 21 Nov 2024. The government has not announced a special sitting, raising questions on procedural compliance and legislative backlog.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Parliament & Legislative Process

Key Data & Stats: Adjourned sine die: 13 Aug 2024, Unprorogued duration: >100 days, Current date: 21 Nov 2024

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Monsoon session, traditionally held in July‑August, concluded without a formal prorogation. Prorogation (Article 85(2) of the Constitution) formally ends a session and clears the legislative calendar. The decision to keep the session unprorogued stems from pending bills, political calculations, and the absence of a consensus on a special sitting.
  • Significance: Without prorogation, the Parliament remains technically in session, limiting the executive’s ability to call a special sitting for urgent legislation (e.g., budgetary approvals, bills on national security). It also creates ambiguity in parliamentary accountability, as committees cannot be reconstituted and the legislative calendar remains in limbo.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Legal ambiguity: No explicit time‑frame for prorogation in the Constitution, leading to potential misuse.
    • Administrative bottleneck: Pending bills may face indefinite delay, affecting policy implementation.
    • Precedent risk: Prolonged unprorogued periods could set a precedent for executive overreach, undermining the spirit of Article 85(2).
    • Ethical concern: Lack of transparency in deciding a special sitting may erode public trust.
  • Way Forward:
    • Form a parliamentary committee (e.g., a joint committee of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) to recommend a statutory time‑limit for prorogation, drawing on the Sarkaria Commission’s emphasis on procedural clarity.
    • Introduce a rule amendment in the Rules of Procedure to mandate that a session be prorogued within 30 days of adjournment sine die, unless a special sitting is formally announced.
    • Utilise the President’s power under Article 85(2) to issue a formal proclamation of prorogation if the executive fails to act within the stipulated period.
    • Ensure periodic reporting to the Parliamentary Standing Committees on pending legislation to avoid backlog accumulation.

Prelims Pointers

  • Article 85(2) – Power of the President to prorogue Parliament on the advice of the Council of Ministers.
  • Adjournment sine die – Indefinite suspension of proceedings without formal conclusion of the session.
  • Prorogation vs. Special Sitting – Prorogation ends a session; a special sitting can be convened under Article 85(2) for urgent business.
  • Constitutional precedent – No fixed time‑limit for prorogation; calls for procedural reforms (Sarkaria Commission, ARC recommendations).
