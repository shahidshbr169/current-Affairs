AI cameras, thermal drone to track elephant movement in Keralam’s Vadasserikkara

21 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Kerala government has formulated a joint action plan that deploys AI‑enabled cameras and thermal‑equipped drones to monitor and track the movement of wild elephants in Vadasserikkara, aiming to curb frequent human‑elephant conflicts through coordinated multi‑departmental intervention.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Environment, Ecology & Biodiversity; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Inter‑departmental coordination, use of technology in administration)

Key Data & Stats: 2024 Vadasserikkara, Kerala AI‑enabled cameras Thermal drones

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Recurrent elephant incursions in the forest‑agricultural fringe of Vadasserikkara have led to crop damage, human injuries, and occasional fatalities. Earlier mitigation relied on traditional methods such as watchtowers and translocation, which proved inadequate.
  • Significance: The initiative exemplifies the integration of cutting‑edge technology in wildlife management, enhancing real‑time surveillance, early warning, and rapid response. It also reflects a shift towards data‑driven governance and inter‑agency collaboration (Forest, Police, Disaster Management, Rural Development).
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Legal: Deployment of surveillance equipment must respect privacy norms under Article 21 and the Right to Information Act.
    • Operational: Ensuring continuous power supply and maintenance of high‑cost drones in remote forest terrain.
    • Ecological: Over‑reliance on technology may sideline community‑based mitigation measures and traditional knowledge.
  • Way Forward:
    • Form a monitoring committee under the Ministry of Environment, Forests & Climate Change (MEFCC) to audit effectiveness and cost‑benefit.
    • Incorporate community liaison cells to blend AI data with local intelligence, as recommended by the National Elephant Action Plan (NEAP).
    • Explore public‑private partnerships for drone maintenance and data analytics, guided by the guidelines of the Committee on Technology in Governance (ARC‑type).
    • Periodically review the action plan under the provisions of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, and amend based on impact assessments.

Prelims Pointers

  • Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 – provisions for mitigation of human‑elephant conflict.
  • Article 48A of the Constitution – duty of the State to protect and improve the environment.
  • National Elephant Action Plan (NEAP) – framework for elephant conservation and conflict management.
  • Use of AI and drone technology in forest surveillance – emerging tool for wildlife management.
