European Union proposes tax on Big Tech to boost budget

21 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: The European Council, led by President Antonio Costa, announced a new fiscal proposal that trims the European Commission’s original plan by €204 billion and introduces a levy on large digital‑platform companies to fund the EU budget.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; GS Paper III – International trade, digital economy and taxation.

Key Data & Agreements: €204 billion budget reduction | EU Digital Services Tax (proposed) | 2024‑25 fiscal year (proposal timeline)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The tax reflects the EU’s push to curb the fiscal dominance of US‑based Big Tech, reshaping global digital governance and potentially heightening EU‑US trade frictions.
  • Impact on India: Indian digital service providers (e.g., Jio Platforms, Paytm) could face higher compliance costs; the move aligns with India’s own Equalisation Levy, offering a precedent for coordinated multilateral digital taxation.
  • Way Forward: India should actively engage in WTO negotiations on digital taxes, seek a bilateral dialogue with the EU to ensure a level playing field, and explore leveraging the tax revenue for digital infrastructure development at home.

Prelims Pointers

  • EU’s Digital Services Tax proposal targets firms with annual global revenues > €750 million.
  • Potential EU‑US trade dispute over digital taxation under the WTO framework.
  • India’s Equalisation Levy (2% on digital services) introduced in 2020, mirroring global trends.
  • EU budgetary shortfall prompting the €204 billion cut and new revenue measures.
