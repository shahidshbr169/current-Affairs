U.S. court dispatches summonses to Doval, Goel in Pannun case

21 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: A U.S. civil court issued summonses to India's National Security Advisor Ajit Doval and former Home Ministry official Sanjay Goel in the Pannun case. Under the Indian Code of Civil Procedure, such foreign summonses can be transmitted to Indian courts and treated as if issued by the foreign court, raising issues of jurisdiction and sovereign immunity.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (foreign judicial cooperation, statutory provisions); GS Paper III – International Relations (extraterritorial jurisdiction, diplomatic implications).

Key Data & Stats: Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 Central Government Notification

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Pannun case stems from a U.S. lawsuit alleging that Indian officials facilitated activities of a separatist group. The U.S. court sought to compel testimony from Doval and Goel, prompting the issuance of summonses.
  • Significance: Demonstrates the operationalisation of the CPC provision for cross‑border service of process, testing the balance between India’s sovereign immunity and international legal cooperation. It also highlights the procedural route for foreign courts to seek redress against Indian officials.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Ambiguities in the CPC regarding the extent of service, potential conflict with the doctrine of sovereign immunity, and diplomatic sensitivities. Lack of a clear, time‑bound mechanism for responding to such summonses may lead to protracted litigation.
  • Way Forward: • Form a high‑level inter‑ministerial committee (e.g., under the Attorney General) to draft detailed SOPs for handling foreign summonses.
    • Consider amending the CPC to specify timelines and safeguard provisions for officials performing official duties.
    • Strengthen India‑U.S. Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty (MLAT) provisions to streamline cooperation while preserving sovereign interests.

Prelims Pointers

  • Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 – provisions for service of foreign court summons.
  • Central Government’s power to notify foreign courts whose orders are enforceable in India.
  • Doctrine of sovereign immunity – limits on jurisdiction over serving officials.
  • Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty (MLAT) – framework for cross‑border legal cooperation.
