Karnataka to make fresh bid to secure ST status for Kadugolla, Hattigolla communities

21 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: On 19 Nov 2024, the Karnataka Cabinet reiterated its recommendation to the Union Government for granting Scheduled‑Tribe (ST) status to the Kadugolla and Hattigolla communities, citing fresh evidence gathered through a comprehensive ethnographic study. The move seeks to secure constitutional safeguards and development benefits for these tribal groups.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (tribal welfare, constitutional provisions for STs); GS Paper III – Social Justice, Human Development.

Key Data & Stats: 19 Nov 2024 (Cabinet decision); Ethnographic study – fresh evidence (basis for renewed recommendation).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Kadugolla and Hattigolla communities have long sought ST recognition. Earlier petitions were rejected by the Centre on grounds of insufficient proof of “primitive traits, distinctive culture and geographical isolation” as stipulated under Article 342, Constitution of India. The Karnataka government commissioned a detailed ethnographic survey to address these gaps.
  • Significance: ST status would entitle the communities to 7.5 % reservation in education and public employment, access to the Tribal Sub‑Plan (TSP) funds, and other welfare schemes administered by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs. It also strengthens the federal principle of cooperative federalism where states recommend groups for central recognition.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Ensuring the ethnographic data meets the stringent criteria set by the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) and the Ministry of Tribal Affairs.
    • Potential inter‑state disputes if neighboring states claim overlapping tribal identities.
    • Political sensitivities surrounding reservation expansion and fiscal implications for central schemes.
    • Legal scrutiny under the Supreme Court’s pronouncements on the “socially and economically backward” criteria for ST classification.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a joint expert committee comprising members of the ARC, NCST, and state anthropologists to validate the study’s methodology.
    • Submit a detailed dossier to the Ministry of Tribal Affairs referencing Article 342 and prior SC judgments (e.g., State of Madhya Pradesh v. Rameshwar Prasad).
    • Implement a monitoring mechanism post‑recognition to assess the impact of reservation and development programmes.
    • Engage with the Centre through the Inter‑State Council to pre‑empt any jurisdictional conflicts.

Prelims Pointers

  • Constitutional provision for ST list – Article 342.
  • Key statutory body – National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST).
  • Reservation quota for STs – 7.5 % in education and public employment.
  • Central scheme for tribal development – Tribal Sub‑Plan (TSP).
