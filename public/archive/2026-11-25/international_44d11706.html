Former members say EPG report was ‘highly positive’ for India-Nepal ties

25 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: Former members of the India‑Nepal Eminent Persons Group (EPG) described its report as “highly positive” for bilateral ties, a day after Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri dismissed the report as “not relevant”. The contrasting reactions highlight divergent views on the utility of the EPG’s recommendations for India‑Nepal relations.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; India’s neighbourhood policy; India‑Nepal strategic and economic cooperation.

Key Data & Agreements: EPG report 2024

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The EPG, set up under the 2015 India‑Nepal Treaty of Peace and Friendship, seeks to address long‑standing border, trade, and connectivity issues. A “highly positive” assessment could bolster India’s soft power in the Himalayas, countering China’s growing influence in Nepal.
  • Impact on India: Positive framing of the report may facilitate smoother implementation of cross‑border projects (e.g., road links, energy cooperation) and reinforce India’s role as Nepal’s primary development partner. Conversely, the Foreign Secretary’s dismissal signals possible bureaucratic hesitancy, which could delay policy action.
  • Way Forward: • Re‑engage the EPG to prioritize actionable items and set clear timelines.
    • Align EPG recommendations with existing bilateral mechanisms (e.g., Joint Working Group, Border Management).
    • Use diplomatic outreach to assure Nepal of India’s commitment while managing domestic sensitivities.

Prelims Pointers

  • India‑Nepal Eminent Persons Group (EPG) – established under the 2015 Treaty of Peace and Friendship.
  • Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri’s statement reflects a cautious official stance on the EPG’s relevance.
  • India’s strategic interest in Nepal includes countering China’s Belt‑and‑Road initiatives and securing Himalayan frontiers.
  • Key sectors under EPG focus: trade facilitation, connectivity (road, rail, air), energy cooperation, and people‑to‑people contact.
