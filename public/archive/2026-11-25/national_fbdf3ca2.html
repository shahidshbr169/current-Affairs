No Active Local Militants Left In J&K, 65 Pakistani Terrorists Still On Security Radar: Report

25 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: Security officials have declared that there are no active local militants left in Jammu & Kashmir, while 65 Pakistani‑origin terrorists continue to be tracked on India’s security radar based on multi‑agency intelligence inputs.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Internal Security, Centre‑State Relations); GS Paper III – Security, Law & Order, Terrorism.

Key Data & Stats: 65 foreign terrorists identified; No active local militants in J&K (as per latest security assessment).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Decades‑long insurgency in Jammu & Kashmir saw a mix of home‑grown cadres and cross‑border infiltrators. Intensive security operations, the 2019 re‑organisation of the state, and diplomatic pressure on Pakistan have markedly reduced indigenous militant activity.
  • Significance: The shift from local to predominantly foreign terrorist presence underscores the evolving nature of the threat, impacts India‑Pakistan diplomatic dynamics, and tests the efficacy of India’s intelligence‑co‑ordination mechanisms.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Continuous monitoring of the 65 identified infiltrators demands robust intelligence sharing, legal adjudication under statutes like the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, and safeguards against misuse of powers under AFSPA. Cross‑border infiltration also raises questions on border management and humanitarian considerations.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen joint‑intelligence platforms (e.g., National Intelligence Grid). • Periodic review of AFSPA and its operational guidelines in J&K. • Institutionalise a high‑level committee (e.g., under the National Security Advisory Board) to assess and recommend de‑radicalisation and rehabilitation measures for any residual elements. • Pursue confidence‑building measures with Pakistan while maintaining a firm stance on infiltration.

Prelims Pointers

  • Local militants vs. foreign terrorists – distinction in security terminology.
  • Article 355 of the Constitution – Union’s duty to protect states.
  • AFSPA (Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act) – its relevance in J&K post‑reorganisation.
  • National Investigation Agency (NIA) & Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA) – legal framework for prosecuting foreign terrorists.
