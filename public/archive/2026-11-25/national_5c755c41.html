Mobile wildlife forensic lab to be stationed at Pethikuttai in Coimbatore district

25 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Government of Tamil Nadu will establish a dedicated mobile wildlife forensic laboratory at Pethikuttai in Coimbatore district to facilitate rapid on‑site analysis of wildlife crime evidence, thereby strengthening enforcement of the Wildlife Protection Act.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Polity & Administration; GS Paper III – Environment, Ecology & Biodiversity (Wildlife Conservation, Legislation & Enforcement)

Key Data & Stats: Coimbatore district, Tamil Nadu

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: India records one of the highest incidences of wildlife trafficking globally. Existing forensic facilities are limited to a few static labs, causing delays in evidence processing and low conviction rates. Mobile labs have been piloted in states like Karnataka to bridge this gap.
  • Significance: A mobile lab enables real‑time DNA, toxicology and ballistics analysis at crime scenes, improving the probative value of evidence, expediting court proceedings, and acting as a deterrent against poaching and illegal trade. It aligns with the objectives of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 and the National Biodiversity Action Plan.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Funding and sustained operational costs; • Need for trained wildlife forensic scientists and technicians; • Coordination between forest department, police, and forensic agencies; • Legal admissibility of mobile‑collected evidence under the Indian Evidence Act, 1872; • Data security and chain‑of‑custody protocols.
  • Way Forward: • Institutionalise a state‑wide network of mobile labs linked to the Central Forensic Science Laboratories (CFSL) for quality control; • Allocate a dedicated budget under the State Forest Department and seek central assistance via the Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change; • Form a joint oversight committee (including members of the Wildlife Crime Control Bureau) to monitor performance; • Conduct periodic capacity‑building programmes in collaboration with the National Institute of Criminology and Forensic Science (NICFS); • Recommend amendment of the Wildlife (Protection) Act to expressly recognise mobile forensic units as “authorized forensic facilities.”

Prelims Pointers

  • Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 – key provisions for offence investigation and evidence handling.
  • Article 48A of the Constitution – State’s duty to protect and improve the environment.
  • Forensic Science Laboratories (Establishment) Act, 2003 – framework for setting up forensic labs.
  • Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB) – nodal agency for wildlife crime investigation.
