Govt. introduces price stabilisation scheme to curb price rise

25 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: The Government has launched a Price Stabilisation Scheme aimed at curbing the recent surge in consumer prices by intervening in markets and providing targeted subsidies. The move seeks to protect purchasing power and ensure price stability for essential commodities.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Economic Development, Agriculture & Food Management; GS Paper II – Governance, Policy Implementation, Statutory Bodies

Key Data & Stats:

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Persistent inflationary pressures on food and essential items have heightened consumer concerns. Earlier ad‑hoc measures (e.g., price caps, buffer stock releases) proved insufficient, prompting a structured scheme.
  • Significance: Stabilising prices directly impacts the CPI‑based inflation trajectory, safeguards low‑income households, and reduces the fiscal cost of high interest rates for the central bank.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Potential fiscal strain on the exchequer; risk of market distortion if subsidies are not time‑bound; coordination challenges between Centre and States; legal scrutiny under the Essential Commodities Act and the Constitution’s Division of Powers (Article 246).
  • Way Forward: Institutionalise the scheme through a dedicated inter‑ministerial committee (e.g., NITI Aayog‑led); set clear exit criteria and periodic review; leverage technology for real‑time price monitoring; involve state governments via the Finance Commission mechanism; ensure transparency through parliamentary oversight.

Prelims Pointers

  • Price Stabilisation Scheme (PSS) – a centrally‑funded market‑intervention mechanism.
  • Essential Commodities Act, 1955 – legal framework for regulating supply and prices of essential items.
  • Consumer Price Index (CPI) – primary indicator for measuring inflation impact on households.
  • Role of NITI Aayog in policy design and inter‑governmental coordination.
