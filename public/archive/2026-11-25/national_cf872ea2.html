Aero India: Record exhibitor demand prompts capacity expansion by Ministry of Defence

25 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: The Ministry of Defence announced an expansion of the Aero India exhibition’s infrastructure after receiving an unprecedented number of exhibitor applications, signalling heightened interest in India’s defence and aerospace sector.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Defence, Security and International Relations; GS Paper II – Governance, Policy Implementation, Public Administration

Key Data & Stats: 2024 (Aero India edition) record exhibitor demand

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Aero India, a biennial air show organised by the Ministry of Defence, showcases indigenous and foreign defence technologies. Over the past editions, the event has become a platform for the “Make in India” drive in the aerospace sector.
  • Significance: Expanding exhibition capacity reflects growing domestic and international confidence in India’s defence procurement ecosystem, aids technology transfer, and supports the strategic objective of self‑reliance (Atmanirbhar Bharat) in defence.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Rapid infrastructure scaling may strain existing security clearances, logistics, and coordination with civil aviation authorities. Ensuring equitable access for MSMEs while managing foreign participation raises policy and regulatory concerns.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute a dedicated Aero India Authority under the MoD for long‑term planning (akin to the Airports Authority model). • Adopt a public‑private partnership (PPP) framework for infrastructure development. • Align exhibition guidelines with the Defence Production Policy 2022 and the recent Defence Procurement Procedure (DPP) revisions. • Periodic review by the Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) to monitor capacity utilisation and stakeholder feedback.

Prelims Pointers

  • Aero India – biennial air show and defence exhibition organised by the Ministry of Defence.
  • Make in India & Defence Production Policy – key initiatives driving indigenous aerospace manufacturing.
  • Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) – apex body overseeing major defence procurement and exhibition policies.
  • Public‑Private Partnership (PPP) model – recommended for large‑scale infrastructure projects in the defence sector.
