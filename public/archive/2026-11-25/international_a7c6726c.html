U.S. set to slap tariffs on China, EU and others after probe on excess industrial capacity: report

25 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: The United States is preparing to impose new tariffs on China, the European Union and 13 other major trading partners, including India, following an investigation into excess industrial capacity. The move revives the “Liberation Day” tariffs championed by former President Trump, which were previously struck down by the U.S. Supreme Court.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; International Trade & Economic Relations; WTO and trade dispute mechanisms.

Key Data & Agreements: 16 major U.S. trading partners named | Tariffs targeting excess industrial capacity | “Liberation Day” tariffs (revived)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: The tariffs signal a shift toward protectionist policies by the U.S., challenging the post‑World War II liberal trade order and potentially prompting retaliatory measures from affected blocs, thereby reshaping global supply chains.
  • Impact on India: While India is not the primary target, inclusion among the 16 partners may raise the cost of U.S.‑origin inputs, affect export competitiveness, and compel Indian firms to reassess sourcing strategies from the U.S. and allied markets.
  • Way Forward: India should engage diplomatically through WTO channels, diversify trade partners, strengthen domestic manufacturing under the “Make in India” agenda, and negotiate sector‑specific exemptions or mitigation measures.

Prelims Pointers

  • U.S. tariffs on China, EU and 13 other partners – a total of 16 major trading partners.
  • Tariff rationale: alleged excess industrial capacity in targeted economies.
  • Revival of Trump’s “Liberation Day” tariffs previously invalidated by the Supreme Court.
  • Potential WTO dispute as the measure may contravene multilateral trade commitments.
