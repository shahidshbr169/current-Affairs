Parents who work for children’s future end up in old age homes, says writer

12 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: A writer highlighted that many parents who over‑exert themselves to secure their children’s future end up in old‑age homes, a concern echoed in a Children’s Day programme that called for preserving cultural values and curbing academic pressure and excessive phone use among children.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Social Justice, Welfare, Human Development

Key Data & Stats: 4.9% of India’s population aged 60+ (Census 2011) 8% of senior citizens live alone (National Sample Survey, 2022‑23) Article 21A – Right to Education Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Rapid socio‑economic change, rising living costs and competitive education systems have intensified parental pressure to invest heavily in children’s education and career, often at the expense of their own financial security. Simultaneously, cultural erosion and digital addiction among children have prompted civil‑society interventions on Children’s Day.
  • Significance: The issue touches upon inter‑generational equity, senior‑citizen welfare, child rights, and the effectiveness of existing social‑security frameworks. It also raises questions on the adequacy of policy mechanisms like the Senior Citizens’ Welfare Scheme and the Right to Education in safeguarding both elders and children.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Inadequate implementation of the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007 – low awareness and weak enforcement.
    • Limited financial inclusion for informal‑sector workers who constitute most of these parents.
    • Absence of a comprehensive national child‑well‑being index to monitor academic pressure and digital exposure.
    • Cultural shift away from joint‑family support systems, increasing reliance on institutional care.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen the implementation of the Senior Citizens’ Act through a dedicated monitoring cell under the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment.
    • Introduce a “Parental Pension Linked to Child Education Savings” scheme under the PM‑Kisan/PM‑JEEVAN framework to ensure parents’ financial security.
    • Constitute a high‑level committee (modeled on the ARC) to draft a National Child Well‑Being Policy integrating mental‑health, digital‑usage guidelines, and cultural‑value curricula.
    • Promote community‑based elder care models and incentivise joint‑family arrangements via tax benefits.

Prelims Pointers

  • Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007 – provides legal recourse for children to maintain parents.
  • Article 21A of the Constitution – guarantees free and compulsory education for children aged 6‑14.
  • National Policy for Senior Citizens (2011) – outlines health, social security, and welfare measures for the elderly.
  • Children’s Day (14 Nov) – platform for governmental and civil‑society discourse on child protection, cultural values, and digital safety.
