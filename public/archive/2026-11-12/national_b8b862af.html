Odisha to build first large-scale pumped storage project for ₹4,708 crore

12 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Odisha will construct a 600 MW Upper Indravati pumped‑storage hydro project at an estimated cost of ₹4,708 crore, slated for commissioning in FY 31. The facility will use the existing reservoir to store surplus renewable energy, thereby strengthening grid stability and aiding India’s renewable‑energy integration goals.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (energy policy, inter‑state power coordination); GS Paper III – Environment, Ecology & Biodiversity; Science & Technology (renewable energy storage).

Key Data & Stats: 600 MW capacity | ₹4,708 crore investment | FY 31 commissioning | First large‑scale pumped storage in India

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: India’s renewable‑energy target of 450 GW by 2030 and frequent curtailment of solar & wind have created a pressing need for large‑scale, grid‑scale storage. Pumped‑storage hydro (PSH) is the most mature technology for bulk energy storage, and the National Electricity Plan (2023) explicitly encourages PSH projects to balance intermittent supply.
  • Significance: The Upper Indravati PSH will provide peak‑shaving, frequency regulation and reserve power, reducing reliance on costly thermal peaking plants. It will enhance the viability of renewable projects in the Eastern and Central zones, supporting the country’s climate‑mitigation commitments under the Paris Agreement.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • High upfront capital cost and long gestation period.
    • Requirement of clearances under the Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act, Forest (Conservation) Act and potential displacement of local communities.
    • Need for a transparent tariff framework (e.g., capacity‑based vs. energy‑based tariffs) to attract private participation.
    • Inter‑state transmission constraints for evacuating stored power to load centres.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute a multi‑ministerial task‑force (Ministry of Power, MoEFCC, NITI Aayog) to fast‑track clearances and formulate a standardised PSH policy.
    • Adopt a PPP model with viability gap funding, similar to the UDAY scheme for power distribution.
    • Align the project with the Green Energy Corridor and incentivise ancillary services markets for frequency regulation.
    • Periodic review by the Central Electricity Authority (CEA) to monitor performance and guide replication in other river basins.

Prelims Pointers

  • Pumped‑storage hydro (PSH) – a mature bulk‑energy storage technology.
  • Upper Indravati pumped‑storage project – 600 MW, ₹4,708 crore, FY 31.
  • National Electricity Plan 2023 – promotes PSH for renewable integration.
  • Key regulatory bodies: Ministry of Power, Central Electricity Authority (CEA), Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change (MoEFCC).
