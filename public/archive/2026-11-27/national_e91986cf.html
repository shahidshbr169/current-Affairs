Thirupparankundram row highlights: Supreme Court orders deepam to be lit on 'deepathoon' on November 28

27 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Supreme Court has directed that the ceremonial lamp (deepam) at the Thirupparankundram temple be lit on the designated “deepathoon” on 28 Nov 2026. It observed that the Tamil Nadu administration – including the Chief Secretary, DGP, HR&CE Commissioner and the temple’s Board of Trustees – “wilfully disobeyed” earlier court orders and issued contempt show‑cause notices against them.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Law and Justice (Contempt of Courts Act, 1971; Article 32, 141).

Key Data & Stats: 28 Nov 2026 – Deepam lighting date, 5 Officials – Show‑cause notices issued, Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 – Section 2 (definition of contempt)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Thirupparankundram temple, a historic Shaivite shrine, has been at the centre of a long‑standing dispute over the timing of the annual deepam ceremony. The Supreme Court, in earlier judgments, mandated that the lamp be lit on the specific “deepathoon” to preserve religious tradition and prevent communal friction. The Tamil Nadu state machinery repeatedly postponed the ceremony, prompting contempt petitions.
  • Significance: The order re‑asserts the supremacy of judicial pronouncements over executive action, reinforcing the rule of law and secular administration of religious endowments under the HR&CE Act. It also underscores the importance of timely compliance to avert communal tensions in a sensitive religious site.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Persistent non‑compliance reflects gaps in inter‑departmental coordination, weak enforcement of contempt provisions, and possible political interference. The episode raises questions about the efficacy of existing mechanisms to ensure that state officials adhere to Supreme Court directives, especially in matters involving religion.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen the monitoring framework for implementation of Supreme Court orders – e.g., periodic compliance reports to the Court.
    • Empower the HR&CE Department with a clear SOP for court‑ordered religious ceremonies, overseen by an independent committee (suggested: a sub‑committee of the Sarkaria Commission on Centre‑State relations).
    • Amend the Contempt of Courts Act to introduce automatic penalties for wilful defiance by public officials, ensuring swift deterrence.
    • Conduct capacity‑building workshops for senior bureaucrats on constitutional duties and judicial compliance.

Prelims Pointers

  • Supreme Court – apex judicial authority; its orders are binding under Article 141, Constitution of India.
  • Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 – Section 2 defines contempt; non‑compliance can attract penal action.
  • HR&CE Department – statutory body governing Hindu religious institutions in Tamil Nadu.
  • Article 32 – right to constitutional remedy for enforcement of fundamental rights, often invoked in contempt petitions.
