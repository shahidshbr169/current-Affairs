ADB-backed scheme may lead to closure of 45,326 govt. schools, says AIDSO

27 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Asian Development Bank‑backed Karnataka Public Schools (KPS) magnet scheme proposes to upgrade a select set of government schools into “public schools”. Student bodies AIDSO and the Save Public Education Committee warn that the restructuring could result in the closure of 45,326 govt. schools, raising concerns over the Right to Education and equitable access.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Education, Social Justice, Welfare of Scheduled Castes/Tribes, Minorities and Women.

Key Data & Stats: 45,326 govt. schools (potential closures); ADB (multilateral funding partner).

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Karnataka government, with ADB assistance, launched the KPS magnet scheme to create a network of high‑quality “public schools” by consolidating resources, upgrading infrastructure, and introducing a merit‑based admission system. The move aligns with broader “learning outcomes” reforms but has triggered opposition from student and civil‑society groups fearing mass school shutdowns.
  • Significance: The proposal touches upon Article 21A of the Constitution (right to free and compulsory education), the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009, and the principle of equitable access to education. Large‑scale school closures could exacerbate regional disparities, increase travel distances for children, and undermine the goal of universal elementary education.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Legal: Potential violation of the RTE Act’s mandate that no child be denied admission in a neighbourhood school.
    • Administrative: Lack of transparent criteria for school selection and inadequate stakeholder consultation.
    • Social: Risk of marginalising vulnerable groups (SC/ST, minorities) who rely on nearby government schools.
    • Financial: While ADB funding supports infrastructure, the cost of relocating students and managing transition is not clearly accounted for.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a multi‑stakeholder committee (including representatives from AIDSO, Save Public Education Committee, education experts, and the State Education Department) modelled on the Kothari Commission to review the scheme.
    • Ensure compliance with Article 21A and RTE Act by mandating that no school closure occurs without provision of an alternative school within 5 km radius.
    • Adopt a phased implementation with pilot clusters, rigorous impact assessment, and a grievance redressal mechanism.
    • Explore alternative financing (e.g., state budget allocations, CSR) to reduce over‑reliance on external loans and safeguard public education.
