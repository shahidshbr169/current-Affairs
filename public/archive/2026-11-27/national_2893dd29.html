HC impleads CBI for assistance over SBI sanctioning ₹500 crore loan to M/s Sem India Fab

27 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The High Court has directed the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) to assist in probing the State Bank of India’s (SBI) sanction of a ₹500 crore loan to M/s Sem India Fab. The order underscores judicial scrutiny of large public‑sector bank disbursements and potential irregularities in corporate financing.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Public‑sector banks, statutory bodies, judicial review); GS Paper III – Economic Development (Banking sector, credit policy).

Key Data & Stats: ₹500 crore loan State Bank of India (SBI) Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Public‑sector banks like SBI are mandated to follow RBI’s credit‑risk norms and prudential guidelines. Large corporate loans often attract scrutiny when due‑diligence, collateral valuation, or repayment capacity appear questionable, prompting judicial intervention.
  • Significance: Judicial direction for CBI involvement signals heightened oversight of financial disbursements, safeguarding public funds and reinforcing confidence in the banking system. It also reflects the role of the judiciary in checking possible misuse of public‑sector credit.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Over‑lap of investigative agencies (CBI vs. Enforcement Directorate), potential delays in loan recovery, and the need for clear demarcation of powers between the judiciary, RBI, and banking regulators. Ensuring that the probe does not undermine the autonomy of the banking sector while maintaining accountability is a delicate balance.
  • Way Forward: • Strengthen RBI’s supervisory audits for high‑value corporate loans.
    • Institutionalise a joint oversight committee comprising the RBI, Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG), and a parliamentary committee on banking reforms.
    • Adopt stricter collateral and cash‑flow verification norms for loans > ₹300 crore.
    • Periodic reporting of large‑value loans to the High Courts to pre‑empt litigation.

Prelims Pointers

  • High Court’s power to direct investigative agencies under Article 226 of the Constitution.
  • CBI’s jurisdiction in cases of alleged financial irregularities involving public‑sector banks.
  • RBI’s prudential guidelines for large corporate lending (e.g., exposure limits, asset classification).
  • State Bank of India – largest public‑sector bank, subject to Parliamentary Committee on Banking Reforms.
