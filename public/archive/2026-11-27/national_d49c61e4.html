ASHAs’ strike: A test of the Congress government’s sensitivity

27 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHAs) across several states have staged a strike demanding regularisation of their employment status, higher pay and social security benefits. Their volunteer designation leaves them with remuneration and welfare entitlements far below those of regular government health workers, testing the Congress‑led government’s responsiveness to frontline health workers.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; GS Paper III – Health, Social Justice and Welfare

Key Data & Stats: Volunteer cadre – no statutory pension Remuneration ~ ₹2,500–₹3,000 per month (varies by state)

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: ASHAs were created under the National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) in 2005 as community‑level health volunteers to promote maternal and child health, immunisation and disease surveillance. Their engagement terms are contractual, without the benefits accorded to regular staff of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
  • Significance: ASHAs are the first point of contact for rural health services, especially in remote and underserved areas. Their strike threatens continuity of essential programmes such as Janani Suraksha Yojana, immunisation drives and COVID‑19 surveillance, thereby impacting public health outcomes and the government’s health‑related SDG targets.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Legal ambiguity: The “volunteer” label exempts the government from statutory obligations under the Employees’ Provident Funds Act, 1952 and the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972.
    • Fiscal constraints: Regularising ASHAs would increase wage bills for state health departments, raising concerns about budgetary allocations under the 15th Finance Commission.
    • Equity issue: Disparities between ASHAs and other health‑sector employees raise questions of vertical equity and morale among frontline workers.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a high‑level committee (similar to the Ashok Jhunjhunwala Committee on health workforce) to examine the legal status of ASHAs and recommend a phased regularisation plan.
    • Introduce a statutory “Community Health Worker” category with defined salary scales, pension, and grievance redressal mechanisms under the Ministry of Health.
    • Leverage the 2025–26 Union Budget to earmark a dedicated “ASHAs Welfare Fund” financed jointly by Centre and States, ensuring fiscal sustainability.
    • Adopt best‑practice models from states like Kerala and Tamil Nadu where ASHAs receive performance‑linked incentives and limited social security benefits.

Prelims Pointers

  • ASHAs – Community health volunteers under the National Rural Health Mission (NRHM).
  • Relevant statutes: Employees’ Provident Funds Act, 1952; Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 (not applicable to volunteers).
  • Key ministries/bodies: Ministry of Health and Family Welfare; National Health Mission; State Health Departments.
  • Constitutional relevance: Article 21 (right to health) and Article 14 (equality) in the context of equitable employment conditions.
