South's share won't shrink: Naidu urges Opposition to back 50% Lok Sabha seat hike

27 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: Union Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu endorsed the Centre’s proposal to increase Lok Sabha strength by 50 % and urged all parties to back the accompanying women’s reservation bill. He asserted that the expansion would keep the southern states’ share of parliamentary seats unchanged while addressing demographic imbalances.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Articles on representation, amendment procedure, and reservation); GS Paper III – Demography & Development (population trends, gender representation).

Key Data & Stats: 50 % increase in Lok Sabha seats | Women’s reservation of 33 % in Lok Sabha | Current southern states’ share ≈ 15 % of total seats

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Lok Sabha currently has 543 elected members (plus 2 nominated from UTs). Periodic reviews by the Delimitation Commission have highlighted disparities arising from uneven population growth across states. The Government’s draft amendment proposes raising the strength to 822 seats and reserving one‑third for women, aiming to reflect demographic realities and improve gender parity.
  • Significance: An enlarged Lok Sabha would recalibrate the centre‑state balance, potentially increasing representation for high‑growth states while preserving the proportional share of the South. Women’s reservation would be a landmark step toward gender‑inclusive governance, aligning with Sustainable Development Goal 5.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Constitutional amendment under Article 368 requires a special majority in both Houses and ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures – a politically sensitive hurdle.
    • Delimitation after the 2026 census is frozen; expanding seats now may clash with the freeze, raising legal questions.
    • Opposition parties fear that a larger Lok Sabha could dilute their influence and increase administrative costs.
    • Implementation of women’s reservation may face resistance from parties citing merit‑based concerns.
  • Way Forward:
    • Constitute a high‑level committee (e.g., chaired by a former Chief Election Commissioner) to examine the constitutional and fiscal implications of the seat hike.
    • Seek consensus through the Inter‑State Council, ensuring that the proposed share for southern states is mathematically preserved (e.g., using the formula in Article 81(1)).
    • Phase‑wise implementation: first amend the Representation of People Act, then undertake a fresh delimitation exercise post‑2026 census.
    • Parallel public awareness campaign on women’s reservation to build societal support and mitigate political opposition.

Prelims Pointers

  • Article 81(1) – allocation of seats in Lok Sabha to states and union territories.
  • Article 368 – procedure for amendment of the Constitution.
  • Representation of People Act, 1951 – governs elections and seat allocation.
  • Women’s reservation Bill – seeks to reserve 33 % seats for women in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies.
