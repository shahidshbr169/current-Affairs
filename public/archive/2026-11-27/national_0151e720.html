India, US push for trade deal as negotiations continue; no timeline set, says Commerce Secretary

27 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: India and the United States are continuing talks on a bilateral free‑trade agreement (FTA), with Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal emphasizing that while trade ties remain robust, no definitive deadline has been set for concluding the pact. The dialogue reflects both countries’ intent to deepen market access and diversify export‑promotion mechanisms.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper I – Indian Economy (Trade Policy, Export Promotion, FDI); GS Paper III – International Relations (India‑US strategic partnership, bilateral economic agreements)

Key Data & Stats: No fixed timeline for FTA conclusion Commerce Secretary: Rajesh Agrawal

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Negotiations for an India‑US FTA have been intermittent since the 2010s, gaining momentum after the 2020 “Strategic Trade Initiative” and the 2022 “U.S.–India Trade and Investment Framework Agreement”. Both economies seek to reduce tariff barriers, enhance services trade, and address supply‑chain resilience.
  • Significance: A successful FTA could boost Indian exports, attract U.S. investment in high‑tech and manufacturing sectors, and align with the “Make in India” agenda. For the U.S., it offers a reliable partner in the Indo‑Pacific, counterbalancing China’s economic clout.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Divergent tariff reduction schedules, especially in agriculture and textiles.
    • Regulatory harmonisation on standards, data‑privacy, and digital trade.
    • Domestic political pressures in both countries over market‑opening fears.
    • Need to align the agreement with WTO obligations and existing regional trade pacts (e.g., RCEP, ASEAN‑India FTA).
  • Way Forward: • Set up a joint “India‑US Trade Facilitation Committee” (modeled on the Indo‑EU Trade Committee) to resolve sector‑specific disputes.
    • Conduct a comprehensive impact assessment through the Ministry of Commerce’s “Export Promotion Council” to identify vulnerable domestic industries.
    • Involve the NITI Aayog’s Economic Advisory Council for policy calibration.
    • Align the FTA timeline with the “National Trade Policy 2025” rollout to ensure coherence with broader export‑orientation strategies.

Prelims Pointers

  • Free Trade Agreement (FTA) – bilateral trade pact framework.
  • Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal – key negotiator for India.
  • U.S.–India Trade and Investment Framework Agreement (TIFA) – precursor to an FTA.
  • Relevant constitutional provision: Article 301 (Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse throughout the territory of India) and its interaction with international trade agreements.
