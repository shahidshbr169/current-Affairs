Hasina has become a ‘thorn’ in Bangladesh-India ties: Minister Humaiun Kobir

27 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: Indian Foreign Affairs Minister Humaiun Kobir warned that former Bangladeshi Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina’s continued presence in India is increasingly becoming a “thorn” in bilateral ties, signalling growing diplomatic strain between the two neighbours.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; India’s relations with South Asian neighbours; regional security architecture.

Key Data & Agreements: 1972 India‑Bangladesh Treaty of Friendship, 2015 Land Boundary Agreement (LBA), ~$2.5 billion bilateral trade (2023‑24), 1,400 km shared border

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Context & Geopolitical Impact: Bangladesh’s strategic location in the Bay of Bengal makes it pivotal for India’s maritime security, energy corridors and the Indo‑Pacific balance. Any friction can reverberate across SAARC, BIMSTEC and affect China’s outreach in the region.
  • Impact on India: Strained ties risk disruption of cross‑border trade, water‑sharing arrangements (Ganges‑Teesta), and cooperation on Rohingya refugee management. Security cooperation on insurgency‑prone border districts may also be compromised.
  • Way Forward: India should pursue calibrated diplomatic engagement – senior‑level dialogues, confidence‑building measures on trade and water sharing, and joint infrastructure projects (e.g., rail‑link upgrades). Parallelly, India can leverage multilateral platforms like BIMSTEC to institutionalise dispute‑resolution mechanisms.

Prelims Pointers

  • Bangladesh‑India border length: 1,400 km.
  • Annual bilateral trade (FY 2023‑24): approx. $2.5 billion, with India enjoying a trade surplus.
  • Key bilateral frameworks: 1972 Treaty of Friendship, 2015 Land Boundary Agreement, 2022 MoU on connectivity (rail & road).
  • Regional groupings: Both are members of SAARC, BIMSTEC and the Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA).
