'Need to treasure those': SC refers 135-year-old library relocation case to CJI; no shifting of books for now

20 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Supreme Court, hearing a suo motu petition on the proposed relocation of Delhi’s 135‑year‑old Central Secretariat Library, has ordered that the matter be placed before the Chief Justice of India for allocation to a suitable bench. No immediate shifting of the library’s collection has been permitted.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity; Heritage Conservation; Judicial Review & Suo Motu Powers of the Supreme Court.

Key Data & Stats: 135‑year‑old library Supreme Court Suo Motu Petition Referral to CJI for bench listing

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Central Secretariat Library, established in the late 19th century, houses a rare collection of government documents, historical manuscripts and reference material. A proposal to shift the library to a new premises within the Secretariat complex triggered concerns over preservation, accessibility and procedural propriety, prompting the Supreme Court’s suo motu intervention.
  • Significance: The case underscores the judiciary’s role in safeguarding cultural heritage and public assets, and highlights the need for transparent decision‑making in government‑initiated relocations. It also raises issues of administrative accountability under the Constitution’s directive principles concerning preservation of monuments and historic sites.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: • Lack of a clear statutory framework governing relocation of heritage libraries.
    • Potential conflict between administrative efficiency (space optimisation) and conservation norms.
    • Absence of stakeholder consultation (library users, historians, conservation experts).
    • Judicial over‑reach concerns versus legitimate suo motu jurisdiction to protect public interest.
  • Way Forward: • Constitute a high‑level committee (e.g., under the Ministry of Culture) comprising heritage experts, archivists and urban planners to assess feasibility.
    • Apply provisions of the Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972 and the National Heritage Act, 2003 for any structural changes.
    • Ensure compliance with the Supreme Court’s directive: no relocation until a detailed impact‑assessment report is placed before the bench.
    • Institutionalise a protocol for inter‑departmental coordination on heritage assets, possibly via a standing committee under the Cabinet Secretariat.

Prelims Pointers

  • Supreme Court’s suo motu jurisdiction – exercised under Article 32/Article 141 for protection of public interest.
  • Central Secretariat Library – a 135‑year‑old heritage institution under the ambit of the Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972.
  • Referral to the Chief Justice of India for bench allocation – procedural step in high‑profile cases.
  • Heritage conservation vs. administrative restructuring – a recurring theme in governance and urban planning.
