Action sought against author for his controversial remarks on Basavanna

20 November 2026•1 min read

Core Summary: An author has been booked for allegedly making offensive remarks about Basavanna, a revered 12th‑century social reformer of the Lingayat community. The complaint seeks legal action, raising questions on the limits of free speech vis‑à‑vis religious sentiments.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Fundamental Rights, Freedom of Speech, Secularism); GS Paper III – Law and Order (Criminal Law, Hate‑speech provisions).

Key Data & Stats: Article 19(1)(a) – Freedom of Speech & Expression Article 19(2) – Reasonable Restrictions Section 295A IPC – Deliberate and malicious acts intended to outrage religious feelings Section 153A IPC – Promoting enmity between different groups

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Basavanna is the founder of the Lingayat faith, which has been seeking separate religious status. Remarks perceived as derogatory have historically triggered protests and legal actions in Karnataka.
  • Significance: The case tests the balance between Article 19(1)(a) and the permissible restrictions under Article 19(2). It also highlights the sensitivity of religious sentiments in a pluralistic society and the potential for communal friction.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Determining intent behind the remarks, preventing misuse of hate‑speech provisions, safeguarding literary freedom, and ensuring that legal action does not become a tool for political vendetta.
  • Way Forward: • A Law Commission‑led review of Sections 295A & 153A to delineate clear parameters for hate speech.
    • Judicial guidelines to ensure that criminal defamation is invoked only after a thorough inquiry.
    • Strengthening community‑engagement mechanisms to address grievances before litigation.

Prelims Pointers

  • Fundamental Right – Article 19(1)(a) & 19(2) of the Constitution.
  • Criminal provisions – Section 295A & Section 153A of the Indian Penal Code.
  • Key SC judgment – Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) on internet free speech and reasonable restrictions.
  • Lingayat community’s demand for separate religious status – a recurring socio‑political issue.
