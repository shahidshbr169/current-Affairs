Court order on Mayilpeeli examination direct consequence of UDF govt.’s pro-Sangh agenda: Pinarayi Vijayan

20 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: The Kerala High Court revoked its earlier permission for the Mayilpeeli teacher‑recruitment examination, a move that Chief Minister Pinarayi Vijayan says is part of the UDF government’s strategy to hand over the education sector to RSS‑linked outfits. He points to appointments from the National Adhyapaka Parishad and a BJP member on the Curriculum Committee as evidence of a pro‑Sangh agenda.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Education policy, judicial review, federalism, constitutional provisions on minority institutions)

Key Data & Stats: Court order revoking permission National Adhyapaka Parishad appointment BJP member on Curriculum Committee

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: The Mayilpeeli examination is a state‑run recruitment test for teachers. The previous UDF‑led government granted permission for the exam, but the High Court later cancelled it, citing procedural irregularities. The ruling coincides with allegations that the government is facilitating RSS‑affiliated bodies (e.g., National Adhyapaka Parishad) to influence curriculum and staffing.
  • Significance: Control over teacher recruitment and curriculum design directly impacts the ideological orientation of the education system. A shift towards RSS‑linked institutions raises concerns about secularism, minority rights under Article 30, and the federal balance between state and central education policies.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis: Legal challenges revolve around the validity of the court’s reversal and whether appointments violate the principle of merit‑based recruitment. Ethically, the involvement of partisan bodies may erode public trust and contravene the constitutional guarantee of non‑discrimination in education.
  • Way Forward: • Ensure transparent, merit‑based selection through an independent committee (e.g., a reconstituted Selection Board). • Refer to the Sarkaria Commission recommendations on centre‑state cooperation in education to safeguard autonomy while preventing partisan capture. • If needed, seek a stay on the court order pending a detailed review by a larger bench, emphasizing constitutional safeguards under Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of speech & expression) and Article 21 (right to education).

Prelims Pointers

  • Mayilpeeli examination – state‑level teacher recruitment test.
  • National Adhyapaka Parishad – RSS‑affiliated religious/educational body.
  • Curriculum Committee – includes a BJP member, indicating political representation.
  • Article 30 of the Indian Constitution – rights of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions.
