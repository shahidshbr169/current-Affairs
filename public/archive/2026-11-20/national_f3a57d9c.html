India's growth story intact at 7%, but energy costs and twin deficits cloud the outlook

20 November 2026•2 min read

Core Summary: India’s economy is projected to maintain a robust 7% growth driven by domestic demand, infrastructure outlays and manufacturing, but rising energy costs and widening fiscal and current‑account deficits pose risks, with inflation likely to slip beyond the RBI’s 4% ±2% target band.

UPSC Syllabus Mapping: GS Paper III – Economic Development, Inclusive Growth, Government Budgeting; GS Paper II – Governance, Constitution, Polity (Fiscal Federalism, RBI’s mandate)

Key Data & Stats: 7% GDP growth (FY 2024‑25 estimate), Fiscal deficit projected > 5% of GDP, Current‑account deficit widening to ~2.5% of GDP, Inflation expected to breach 6%+

Deep-Dive Analysis

  • Background: Post‑COVID recovery, strong consumption, the 2023‑24 Union Budget’s emphasis on capital spending, and a resurgence in manufacturing (Make in India) have kept growth momentum high. Simultaneously, global commodity price volatility—especially crude oil—has escalated import bills, straining the external sector.
  • Significance: Sustained high growth underpins employment generation and fiscal revenue, but persistent twin deficits can erode macro‑stability, limit fiscal space for social schemes, and force the RBI into a tighter monetary stance, affecting credit flow to the real sector.
  • Challenges / Critical Analysis:
    • Energy import dependence raises vulnerability to global price shocks.
    • Fiscal deficit above the 4.5% of GDP ceiling set by the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act hampers fiscal consolidation.
    • Current‑account deficit expansion pressures foreign exchange reserves and may affect the rupee’s stability.
    • Balancing inflation control with growth support creates a policy dilemma for the RBI under its dual mandate (price stability & financial stability).
  • Way Forward:
    • Accelerate renewable‑energy capacity (solar, wind) to curb oil import bills – align with the National Solar Mission.
    • Implement ARC‑recommended fiscal reforms: broaden tax base, rationalise subsidies, and improve public‑expenditure efficiency.
    • Adopt a calibrated monetary stance: use the RBI’s policy rate to anchor inflation expectations while allowing targeted liquidity for priority sectors.
    • Strengthen external‑sector resilience by diversifying export baskets and promoting services exports, in line with the “Make in India – Export in India” strategy.

Prelims Pointers

  • RBI’s inflation target: 4% ±2% (Article 246‑II of the Constitution empowers Parliament to enact the RBI Act).
  • Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act – fiscal deficit ceiling 4.5% of GDP.
  • Current‑account deficit – a key indicator of external sector health; target generally ≤2% of GDP.
  • Amundi Investment Institute – foreign asset manager providing the growth outlook; reflects global investor sentiment on India.
